Mexico Tax Residency: Why 183 Days Is Not the Test

Mexico’s domestic residence rules focus on your home and, where you have a home abroad too, your centre of vital interests. A visitor allowance is a separate matter. Check your own dates in the calculator below.

Last verified: 19 November 2026

Start with Article 9

Mexico’s Federal Fiscal Code treats individuals who establish their home in Mexico as resident. When they also have a home in another country, the Mexican centre-of-vital-interests test applies. The statute lists Mexican-source income exceeding half of total annual income and the main centre of professional activities in Mexico among the relevant cases.

Nationality and other circumstances matter

The code also contains a rebuttable presumption for Mexican nationals and specific provisions for government personnel. The broad statement that anyone becomes resident only after 183 days therefore misses the actual domestic test.

Do not borrow a tourist limit

Immigration permission determines whether and for how long you may remain as a visitor. Tax residence concerns your home and ties. A visitor permission or a travel count below 183 does not answer the tax question.

Use the record as evidence

Keep an annual log of visits together with information about homes and professional activities. Someone with homes in Mexico and abroad should not interpret 100 Mexican days as proof of non-residence. The log documents presence; the rest of the circumstances still need assessment.

Separate the three questions

May I stay?
Read your immigration permission and its conditions.
Where did I spend time?
Build a physical-presence record from your actual visits.
Where am I tax resident?
Assess the home and vital-interests rules with the other relevant facts.

One document or number rarely answers all three. In particular, an immigration card does not turn the domestic tax test into the number of days that card permits.

Why the same annual total is not a verdict

Illustrative comparison: one person records 150 Mexican days while maintaining homes in Mexico and abroad and conducting their main professional activities in Mexico. Another records 150 days of visits but has a different home and work arrangement.

The identical total does not remove the need to examine the different facts. Nor does owning a property, by itself, answer every question about whether a home has been established and where vital interests lie.

Build the record around a change of circumstances

If you moved during the year, keep the dates that housing arrangements and professional activities changed, not just the flight date. List the homes you had available in each country and the information needed to examine your income and professional ties. Keep any residence-related filings or certificates with the timeline.

The counter below is useful for checking that your visits are complete and consistent. Leaving its limit unset avoids turning a convenient number into a presumed Mexican tax rule. If you choose a personal cap for travel planning, keep that purpose separate from the residence assessment.

Count your recorded days

Use this as a physical-presence record. It does not assess the residence tests described above. Planned stays you enter are included; an optional limit or target is your personal choice.

FAQ

Is there a general Mexican 183-day residence test?

Article 9 uses home and centre-of-vital-interests criteria rather than a general 183-day threshold.

Does my tourist permission decide my tax residence?

No. Immigration status and tax residence are separate.

Can a day log still help?

Yes. It documents physical presence, but it should be considered with housing, income and professional ties.

Official source

Federal Fiscal Code, Article 9.
