Germany Tax Residency: The Six-Month Rule and Trips Abroad

If one continuous stay in Germany lasts more than six months, German law generally treats Germany as your habitual abode from the first day of that stay. Short trips abroad can count toward those six months. Check your own dates in the calculator below.

What happens when your stay exceeds six months?

Under section 9 of the German Fiscal Code (Abgabenordnung, or AO), measure the time from the start of a continuous stay in Germany. Once the stay exceeds six calendar months, Germany treats you as having a habitual abode (gewöhnlicher Aufenthalt) there from the beginning of the stay. Exactly six months does not cross this threshold. The same stay can run across 1 January. There is a specific exception for exclusively private visits, explained below.

This matters because having a habitual abode in Germany generally makes you subject to unlimited German income tax liability under section 1(1) of the Income Tax Act. A home in Germany is a separate way to meet that domestic test. If another country also treats you as resident, the applicable tax treaty is a separate question.

Does a weekend away or a two-week holiday count?

If the trip is a short interruption of the same German stay, the time abroad counts toward the six months. The clock keeps running while you are away. The Federal Fiscal Court expressly confirmed this in case I R 26/10. Your travel record should still show where you actually spent those days.

Suppose you are working on an assignment in Germany, go home to see family for a weekend, and return to the same accommodation and assignment on Monday. That is the kind of interruption the Finance Ministry describes. Its guidance also lists annual leave and business trips. A two-week holiday during the same assignment can qualify on the same basis. There is no automatic 14-day cutoff: the court looks at the length of the break in relation to the whole stay and the circumstances connecting the periods on either side.

In the court case, a presenter returned to Switzerland between production weeks. Her contract called for her to come back to Germany to make the same show over several years. Even the scheduled summer break of roughly six to eight weeks qualified as a short interruption in those circumstances. The continuing contract and the length of the overall engagement explain that result. Eight weeks is no general allowance.

Now change one fact: your assignment ended before you left, and you later accepted a new job in Germany. Even if the gap was also two weeks, the reason to treat the two visits as one stay is much weaker. When reviewing your dates, record what you returned to and whether that return was already arranged when you left.

Worked example: 177 days in Germany and a stay longer than six months

You arrive for work on 1 January 2026 and finish the assignment on 10 July. You spend 14 whole days abroad on holiday, from 15 through 28 April. You keep your accommodation and return to finish the same assignment.

Your travel record: 191 calendar days from 1 January through 10 July, less 14 days abroad, gives 177 days physically in Germany.

The section 9 calculation: assuming the holiday qualifies as a short interruption, the stay runs from 1 January through 10 July. It exceeds six months. You have a German habitual abode from 1 January, even though the physical count never reaches 183.

When the private-visit exception applies

Section 9 sets aside the automatic six-month rule when the whole stay in Germany is exclusively for visits, recreation, medical treatment of the kind covered by the provision, or similar private purposes, and lasts no longer than one year. An eight-month stay solely to visit family can fall within this exception. Taking two weeks of annual leave during a German work assignment does not turn the assignment into an exclusively private visit.

Can German tax residence begin earlier?

Yes. Section 8 AO asks whether you have a dwelling in Germany under circumstances showing that you will keep and use it. That test has no six-month waiting period. Section 9 also has a general test for habitual abode: do the circumstances show that you are staying somewhere on more than a temporary basis? The six-month provision supplies an automatic answer once its conditions are met. A shorter stay still calls for a look at the facts.

Check your physical days in Germany

Enter your actual German visits below and leave foreign holidays in the travel record as absences. For the example above, enter 1 January through 14 April and 29 April through 10 July 2026. The counter should show 177 days. To assess the six-month rule, keep the start and end of the overall stay alongside that number, with the dates and purpose of each interruption.

FAQ

Does the six-month rule start again on 1 January?

No. Follow the same continuous stay across the year boundary. If it exceeds six months and the automatic rule applies, habitual abode dates back to the start of that stay.

Should I enter a foreign holiday as days in Germany?

Record the holiday abroad. For the separate six-month test, include its duration if it was a short interruption of the same German stay.

Can I rely on staying under 183 physical days?

No. Both a German home and a continuous stay that includes short interruptions can matter even when the physical total is below 183.
