Barbados Tax Residency: 183 Days and the Welcome Stamp

Barbados’s ordinary presence test is more than 182 days in the income year. Ordinary residence is another route, and Welcome Stamp holders have a statutory exception. Check your own dates in the calculator below.

The ordinary presence test

The Barbados Revenue Authority states that individuals spending more than 182 days in the income year are deemed resident. With whole-day counting, the first day meeting that test is day 183. Ordinary residence is a separate basis, so fewer days do not prove non-residence.

The Welcome Stamp exception

Section 4 of the Remote Employment Act treats a qualifying non-national granted a Barbados Welcome Stamp to work remotely as non-resident for the ordinary income-tax residence provision. Do not apply the ordinary day threshold to a valid Welcome Stamp holder as though this exception did not exist.

Keep an annual travel record

Group your visits by calendar year, retain arrival and departure evidence and avoid counting an overlapping day twice. A total of 182 days has not crossed the ordinary numerical threshold; 183 has. The official material reviewed does not specify a general partial-day convention, so check the treatment of travel days when that distinction affects your position.

Residence and the tax bill are different questions

Your ordinary residence, domicile, income sources and any applicable treaty can affect the outcome. A travel log is useful evidence but does not calculate the tax due. Keep the Welcome Stamp and its validity dates with your records if you rely on that exception.

What the boundary looks like in a calendar

Inclusive planning example: 1 January to 30 June 2026 spans 181 calendar days. Including 1 July makes 182; including 2 July makes 183. This illustrates the difference between “more than 182” and “more than 183.” It does not resolve the unconfirmed legal treatment of partial travel days.

When monitoring the ordinary threshold, a counter may show an allowance of 182 days. That is the last whole-day total below the 183-day trigger, not a claim that all circumstances below it are non-resident.

The first question is whether this test applies

Imagine two people with the same 200-day physical-presence record. One relies on the ordinary domestic rules. The other holds a valid Welcome Stamp within the statutory remote-employment treatment. The same number should not be interpreted as the same tax conclusion. The permission and its validity are essential facts for the second person.

Likewise, a person with an ordinary residence connection should not treat a shorter annual log as a complete non-residence argument. Presence, ordinary residence and the Welcome Stamp exception answer different parts of the question.

What to collect before asking for advice

  • An annual arrival-and-departure record, with overlapping dates removed.
  • The Welcome Stamp and relevant validity dates if you rely on it.
  • Information about your home and ordinary residence, rather than only your flights.
  • A list of travel days whose treatment would change whether the numerical threshold is met.

This produces a more useful question than “Am I safe at 182?” It identifies the applicable route, the evidence and the exact counting uncertainty an adviser or the Revenue Authority needs to address.

Check the presence count

This is the ordinary presence count, not the Welcome Stamp treatment or an ordinary-residence assessment. The calculator includes arrival and departure dates as a planning convention; confirm their legal treatment when it matters.

FAQ

Does the rule start at 182 or 183 days?

The ordinary presence test is more than 182 days. On a whole-day basis, that means 183.

Does a Welcome Stamp holder become resident after 183 days?

The Remote Employment Act contains a specific non-residence treatment for qualifying Welcome Stamp holders. The ordinary threshold should not be applied without that exception.

Can fewer days still result in residence?

Yes. Ordinary residence is a separate basis and depends on the relevant circumstances.

Official sources

Barbados Revenue Authority, individual tax residency; Remote Employment Act 2020-23, section 4.
