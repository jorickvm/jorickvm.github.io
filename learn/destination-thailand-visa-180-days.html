Destination Thailand Visa (DTV): How the 180-Day Stay Works

The DTV lets remote workers and people doing Thai cultural activities stay 180 days per entry for five years. Here is how each stay is counted, how the one extension works, and when the days make you a Thai tax resident.

Last verified: October 2026

In short: the Destination Thailand Visa is valid for five years with multiple entries. Each entry allows a stay of up to 180 days, and each entry can be extended once by up to 180 days at an immigration office. After that you have to leave and enter again to start a new stay. Time on a DTV still counts toward tax residence: 180 days in Thailand in a calendar year, across all your entries, makes you a Thai tax resident.

The rule

The DTV is a five-year, multiple-entry visa. It does not give you five years of continuous stay: every entry gives you a permission to stay of up to 180 days, and the visa's validity only decides how long you can keep entering. Three points decide most cases:

  • Each entry starts a new 180-day count. The stamp in your passport shows the last day of that stay.
  • One extension per entry. You can ask an immigration office in Thailand to extend the current stay by up to 180 days, once per entry. The extension is at the officer's discretion and costs THB 1,900 as of September 2026.
  • Then you leave. When the stay and its extension run out, you have to depart and re-enter on the same DTV, while it is still valid, to start a new stay.

The DTV is a visa, not a work permit: a job in Thailand needs a work permit of its own.

Family members

The spouse and children under 20 of a DTV holder

Applicants show at least THB 500,000 in funds. The Royal Thai Embassy in Washington asks for bank statements for the last three months with an ending balance of at least that amount each month.

How the 180 days are counted

The stay runs from the date of entry, and the date on your entry stamp is the one immigration enforces, so check it at the counter. For planning, count the arrival day as day one, which is the cautious reading.

One entry, extended once. You arrive on 1 November 2026. Day one is 1 November, so day 180 is 29 April 2027. An extension of the full 180 days moves the last day to 26 October 2027 at the latest.

A new entry, a new count. If you fly out on 20 March 2027 and come back on 3 April 2027, the new entry gets its own stay of up to 180 days from 3 April, and its own single extension.

The DTV and Thai tax residence

Your visa does not decide your tax status. Thailand counts the days you are in the country in a calendar year, across every entry, and 180 days or more makes you a Thai tax resident for that year. Residents are taxed on foreign income that they bring into Thailand.

A single DTV stay can cross that line on its own if it falls within one calendar year. The stay in the example above splits across two years: 61 days in 2026 and 119 days in 2027 before the extension. The extension, or a second entry later in 2027, can then take the 2027 total past 180. See Thailand's 180-day tax residency rule.

Beyond the day count

Immigration officers decide every admission and every extension, and the embassy that issues your visa sets its own documents and fee. Visitors who do not need a long stay can use the visa exemption instead: see Thailand's 30-day visa exemption. For what an overstay costs, see overstaying a visa or stay limit.

FAQ

How long can I stay in Thailand on a DTV?

Up to 180 days per entry, plus one extension of up to 180 days per entry at an immigration office. To stay longer you have to leave and enter again while the visa is still valid, which starts a new stay.

Can I live in Thailand for five years on a DTV?

Not in one continuous stay. The five years are how long you can keep using the visa to enter. Each entry gives you at most 180 days, or 360 with the extension, before you have to leave.

Do I become a Thai tax resident on a DTV?

If you spend 180 days or more in Thailand in a calendar year, yes, whatever your visa. Days from all your entries in that year are added together.

Official sources

The Royal Thai Embassy in London's Destination Thailand Visa page sets out the five-year validity, the 180-day stay per entry, the single extension and the need to re-enter, and the Royal Thai Embassy in Washington's DTV page lists the categories, the bank-statement requirement and its fee. The extension fee is in the Immigration Bureau's fee schedule (in Thai). Tax residence is defined in the Revenue Department's personal income tax guide.
