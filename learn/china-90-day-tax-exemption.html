China’s 90-Day Tax Exemption for Foreign-Paid Work

Without Chinese domicile, up to 90 full days in a calendar year can qualify foreign-paid employment income for exemption, if no Chinese employer bears the cost.

Last verified: October 2026

If you are domiciled in mainland China, this short-stay exemption does not apply. Without domicile, pay for work there can be exempt when you spend no more than 90 full days in the calendar year, a foreign employer pays it, and no Chinese employer or establishment bears the cost.

Who can qualify
Individuals without domicile in mainland China
Limit
No more than 90 full days
Counting window
1 January to 31 December
Payment condition
Foreign-paid earnings not borne by a Chinese employer or establishment
Legal basis
Individual Income Tax implementing regulations, Article 5; Announcements 2019 Nos. 34 and 35

The rule

The exemption concerns qualifying employment income, not every kind of income you receive. Having your salary transferred from an overseas bank does not settle the question: a Chinese entity may ultimately bear the cost through a recharge or another arrangement.

At 90 days the short-stay condition is still met. Above 90 but below 183 days, pay attributable to work in mainland China is generally taxable whether paid locally or abroad. The change is not limited to earnings for day 91 onward. A tax treaty may provide different relief with its own conditions.

How to count full days

Use complete calendar days for the domestic 90-day residence count. Arrival and departure dates do not count. Hong Kong, Macao and Taiwan are outside this mainland count.

Only complete calendar days count

Arrive on 1 March at 10:00 and leave on 5 March at 18:00. Only 2, 3 and 4 March contain a full 24 hours in mainland China.

Commuter example. You live in Hong Kong and travel to Shenzhen each weekday, returning to Hong Kong every evening. None of those visits contains a full calendar day in mainland China, so they contribute zero full days to this presence count. That does not by itself exempt your earnings from tax.

Do not use this full-day count to apportion taxable salary. The rules for working days are different: for some cross-border roles, a partial mainland day is half a working day.

Beyond the day count

Domicile is habitual residence through household registration, family or economic ties. Without domicile, 183 full days establish tax residence. A further six-year rule can bring foreign-source, foreign-paid income into tax: in a 183-day year, the preceding six years must each reach 183 days without a single absence exceeding 30 days. A shorter year or an absence over 30 consecutive days breaks the sequence.

Directors and senior managers have special rules for Chinese-paid remuneration. The employer, cost allocation and any treaty entitlement still need to be checked separately from days.

FAQ

Is the 90th day allowed?

Yes. The domestic short-stay condition is no more than 90 full days in the calendar year, alongside the domicile and payment conditions.

Is foreign payment alone enough?

No. The income must meet the foreign-employer condition and must not be borne by a Chinese employer or establishment.

Are full days also used to apportion taxable salary?

Not necessarily. The residence-day count and the rules for allocating salary to working days are different.

Official sources

Article 5 of the implementing regulations sets the exemption. Announcement 2019 No. 34 defines residence days; Announcement 2019 No. 35 sets the salary-allocation rules and distinguishes residence days from working days.
