UK ETA vs Standard Visitor Visa: Which One Do You Need?

Your nationality decides it. Visa-exempt visitors need a £20 ETA; visa nationals need a £135 Standard Visitor visa. Both lead to the same visit of up to 6 months, under the same rules.

Last verified: September 2026

In short: if you are from a visa-exempt country, such as the EU states, the US, Canada, Australia or Japan, you need an Electronic Travel Authorisation (ETA): £20, applied for online or in the UK ETA app, usually decided within a day, and valid for 2 years or until your passport expires, whichever comes first. If you are a visa national, such as a citizen of China, India, South Africa or Türkiye, you need a Standard Visitor visa: £135 for up to 6 months, with a decision usually within 3 weeks. Either way, each visit can last up to 6 months, and neither guarantees entry.

Which one applies to you

The UK sorts nationalities into two lists. If yours is on the Visa National List, you need a visa for any visit. If it is on the ETA National List, you need an ETA instead. GOV.UK's "Check if you need a UK visa" tool gives the answer for your passport.

Some people need neither:

  • British and Irish citizens, including dual citizens. A dual British or Irish citizen cannot get an ETA and has to travel on a British or Irish passport, or with a certificate of entitlement.
  • People who already hold UK permission: a UK visa, permission to live, work or study in the UK, or settled or pre-settled status.
  • Residents of Ireland who would otherwise need an ETA, when travelling to the UK from Ireland, Guernsey, Jersey or the Isle of Man. You may need to show proof that you live in Ireland.
  • Airside transit passengers who change planes without passing through UK border control. If you go through passport control to connect, you need an ETA.

A visa-exempt national can still choose to apply for a Standard Visitor visa. GOV.UK suggests it if you have a criminal record or have been refused entry to the UK before, because an ETA refusal cannot be appealed and sends you to the visa route anyway.

The ETA follows your passport

An ETA is tied to the passport you applied with, and you must travel on that passport. It ends on the second anniversary of the grant or when that passport expires, whichever comes first. A new passport means a new ETA.

A short ETA. Your ETA is granted on 10 May 2026 and your passport expires on 1 March 2027. The ETA stops being valid on 1 March 2027, not on 10 May 2028. If you renew your passport in January 2027, the ETA does not transfer, and you apply again before your next trip.

Within its validity, an ETA covers any number of visits. It does not extend how long each visit can last.

What neither one allows

An ETA and a Standard Visitor visa both lead to the same thing: permission to enter as a visitor, decided by a Border Force officer on arrival. The visitor rules in Appendix Visitor apply equally to both, so on either document you cannot:

  • stay longer than 6 months on one visit
  • do paid or unpaid work for a UK company or as self-employed, except for permitted paid engagements and a few similar exceptions
  • claim public funds
  • live in the UK through frequent or successive visits, or make it your main home
  • marry, form a civil partnership, or give notice of either; that needs a Marriage/Civil Partnership Visitor visa

The fourth point is where regular visitors get caught. There is no published number of days per year, but a pattern of long, back-to-back visits can lead to refusal at the border, and GOV.UK warns that a long-term visit visa may be cancelled if your travel history shows you repeatedly living in the UK for extended periods. How that assessment works is covered in the UK Standard Visitor "180-day rule".

Beyond the entry document

The ETA or visa gets you to the border; it does not decide how the UK treats the time you spend there. Long stays can make you UK tax resident under the Statutory Residence Test, which counts days on its own rules, and staying past the date on your permission is an overstay with its own consequences, set out in overstaying a visa or stay limit. The UK is also separate from the Schengen area: UK days do not count toward the Schengen 90/180 rule, and a Schengen visa does not cover the UK.

FAQ

Is a UK ETA a visa?

No. An ETA is a digital permission to travel, linked to your passport. It lets a visa-exempt national board transport to the UK and ask for entry as a visitor. A Border Force officer still decides on arrival whether to let you in.

How much does a UK ETA cost and how long is it valid?

As of September 2026 an ETA costs £20, up from £16 on 8 April 2026. It is valid for 2 years from the grant or until the passport you applied with expires, whichever is sooner, and covers any number of visits of up to 6 months each.

Can I stay longer in the UK with a visa than with an ETA?

Not for an ordinary visit. Both allow up to 6 months per visit under the same visitor rules. A long-term Standard Visitor visa is valid for 2, 5 or 10 years, but each stay is still limited to 6 months. Only specific cases, such as private medical treatment or some academic visits, can be granted longer.

Do EU citizens need an ETA to visit the UK?

Yes. EU citizens have needed an ETA to travel to the UK since 2 April 2025, unless they are also British or Irish citizens or already hold UK permission, such as settled or pre-settled status.

Official sources

GOV.UK sets out how to apply for an ETA, including the fee, decision time and validity, who does not need one and what an ETA does and does not allow. Appendix Electronic Travel Authorisation of the Immigration Rules fixes the 2-year validity and the passport link. The Home Office announced enforcement at boarding from 25 February 2026 and the extension to European visitors from 2 April 2025. Visa fees, the 3-month application window and decision times are on the Standard Visitor visa application page and in the fee table from 8 April 2026. The visitor rules are in Appendix Visitor, and the Visa National List says who needs a visa. GOV.UK's visa checker gives the answer for a specific passport.
