In short: many countries can treat you as tax resident once you spend 183 days there in a tax year. This calculator counts the days you mark per country and calendar year, with the day you arrive and the day you leave both counted, and shows how close each country is to 183.

How the calculator counts

  • Any part of a day counts. The day you arrive and the day you leave are both days in that country. A stay from 1 June to 10 June is ten days.
  • Travel days count for both countries. If you fly from Spain to Portugal on 10 June, that day counts once for Spain and once for Portugal.
  • Calendar years. Days are added up from 1 January to 31 December. The result leads with the country closest to 183 this year and lists the rest below it.
  • Planned stays count too. Mark a trip you have not booked yet to see whether it would take you past 183.

What it does not check

The 183-day count is where most residence tests start, not where they end. Check these against your country's own rule:

  • Midnights instead of days. The UK counts the days you are in the country at midnight, so arrival and departure days do not always count.
  • Tax years that are not calendar years. The UK tax year starts on 6 April, Australia's on 1 July and New Zealand's on 1 April.
  • Rolling periods. New Zealand counts 183 days in any 12-month period, not per tax year.
  • "183 or more" or "more than 183". The exact threshold differs by country, and a few use a different number.
  • Other tests. A home, a family or your main economic interests can make you resident with fewer days, and the US Substantial Presence Test weights days from the two previous years.

The rule itself is explained in 183-Day Tax Residency Rule: How the Count Works, and each country's version is in tax residency by country.

FAQ

Does the day I arrive count as a day in the country?

In this calculator, yes: any part of a day counts, so both the day you arrive and the day you leave are days in that country. Many countries count this way. The UK counts midnights instead, so check your country's rule.

Does spending 183 days in a country make me tax resident?

Often, but not always. Many countries treat 183 days in their tax year as residence, some use a different threshold or period, and other ties such as a home or family can make you resident with fewer days. The calculator shows the count, not the decision.

Is my data stored?

No. The calculator runs in your browser and does not send, log or save the dates you enter. Closing or reloading the page clears them. Import them into AtlasDays to keep them.

Official sources

Spain's tax agency sets residence at more than 183 days in the calendar year, counting sporadic absences. HMRC's Statutory Residence Test guidance counts midnights in a tax year that starts on 6 April. The ATO's 183-day test uses an income year from 1 July, and Inland Revenue New Zealand counts 183 days in any 12-month period. The IRS Substantial Presence Test weights days from the two previous years.
