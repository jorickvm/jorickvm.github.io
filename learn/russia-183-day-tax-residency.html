Russia’s 183-Day Tax Residency Rule

Russia's 183-day rule uses 12 consecutive months during the year, with final status settled over the calendar year. Travel days and some absences count.

Last verified: October 2026

Russia’s Tax Code defines the ordinary presence test as at least 183 calendar days within 12 consecutive months. During-year status uses that rolling period; the tax authority settles final annual status using the calendar year. Both arrival and departure days count.

Threshold
183 calendar days or more
During-year count
12 consecutive months
Final annual status
1 January to 31 December
Travel days
Arrival and departure both count
Legal basis
Tax Code, Article 207(2)

The rule

Two periods matter. The statutory 12-month count is used during the year, including when employment income is paid. At year-end, the Federal Tax Service determines final status from the total for 1 January to 31 December.

A rolling period can therefore cross the threshold before the calendar-year total does. Reaching 183 days in a period spanning two years does not, by itself, establish that the final calendar-year test is met.

How to count it

  1. List your stays in Russia, including entry and exit dates.
  2. Count both travel dates, without counting any date twice.
  3. For a during-year check, total the relevant 12 consecutive months.
  4. For final annual status, total the calendar year separately and review qualifying absences.

Example. You have 100 days in Russia late in one year and 83 early in the next. A rolling 12-month period containing both stays reaches 183. If you have no other countable days in the second year, its final calendar-year total is only 83.

Beyond the day count

Article 207(2) preserves the period for trips abroad lasting less than six months for medical treatment or study. It also covers specified work on offshore hydrocarbon fields. These exceptions mean physical presence alone can understate the legal count; keep supporting records.

Special rules apply to some people serving or working abroad on government assignments. Citizenship alone does not determine ordinary tax residence.

FAQ

Is Russia’s test rolling or calendar-year based?

Article 207 uses 12 consecutive months for during-year status. Final annual status is determined from the calendar-year total.

Do arrival and departure days count?

Yes. Both are days of physical presence in Russia.

Does every trip abroad reduce the legal count?

No. Qualifying treatment or study trips of less than six months and specified offshore work are exceptions under Article 207(2).

Official sources

The Federal Tax Service’s residence guidance and FAQ quote Article 207(2), its absence exceptions and the calendar-year determination. Its archived travel-day clarification confirms inclusion of entry and exit dates; the historical tax rates on that page are not used here.
