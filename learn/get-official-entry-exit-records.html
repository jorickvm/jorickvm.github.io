How to Get Your Official Travel History (Entry and Exit Records)

The exact page to request your entry and exit record in five countries and the Schengen area, and what to do with it next.

Last verified: September 2026

In short: each country records only its own border, so request each one separately from the links below. US visitors get 10 years of I-94 history instantly; the other records take up to a month, or have no published timeline.

Where to request your record

As of September 2026:

US I-94 travel history

Who can use it
Visitors, not US citizens or green card holders
Request it
Search your I-94 travel history
Cost
Free
Wait
Instant
What you get
10 years of US arrivals and departures; most land crossings missing

US CBP records (FOIA)

Who can use it
Anyone; the route for US citizens and green card holders
Request it
File a FOIA request with CBP on SecureReleaseOnline only since 22 January 2026
Cost
Free up to 100 pages and 2 hours of search
Wait
No timeline published
What you get
Your travel to and from the US, back to 1982

Canada Travel History Report

Who can use it
Anyone; skip it for citizenship or PR card applications
Request it
Request your Travel History Report from CBSAAsk for entries and exits
Cost
Free
Wait
Up to 30 days
What you get
Date and port of each entry; exits only if requested

Australia movement records

Who can use it
You, or others with their consent
Request it
Fill in the movement records request form
Cost
No fee stated
Wait
No timeline published
What you get
Every arrival and departure since 1981, all passports; not where you went

EU Entry/Exit System data

Who can use it
Non-EU travelers, for crossings since 12 October 2025
Request it
Contact the EES data controller of a country you visited
Cost
No fee stated
Wait
No timeline published
What you get
Date and place of each Schengen entry and exit, kept 3 years

UK subject access request

Who can use it
Anyone the Home Office holds data on

Free

1 month once documents arrive; delays reported

New Zealand travel movements

Who can use it
You; others need form NZCS 151
Request it
Download form NZCS 150 and email it to Customs
Cost
No fee stated

Up to 20 working days

Arrival and departure dates since the early 1990s

If the record and your memory disagree

  • A crossing is missing. Usually a land border. The next country's entry record fills the gap.
  • A date is off by one. Check overnight flights and time zones; border records use the local date.
  • A trip never happened. Ask for a correction with evidence: DHS TRIP or CBP for I-94, the data controller for EES.

FAQ

How do I get my US travel history as a US citizen?

File a FOIA request with CBP through SecureRelease or FOIA.gov for your records of travel to and from the US. The I-94 site does not cover citizens or green card holders.

Can I get a list of my Schengen entries and exits?

Only for crossings recorded by the Entry/Exit System, which started on 12 October 2025. Ask a data controller in a Schengen country you visited. Earlier trips exist only as passport stamps.

Can I import my official travel history into AtlasDays?

Yes. Convert it to a CSV, for example with the app's Build CSV with AI prompt, and import it under Settings, Import, CSV File. Nothing is saved until you approve the preview.

Official sources

What each record covers, what it costs and how long it takes come from the I-94 help pages, CBP's FOIA FAQ, the CBSA's Travel History Report page, the EU's data held by the EES page, the European Commission's Entry/Exit System page, and the Home Office guidance on requesting your personal information.
