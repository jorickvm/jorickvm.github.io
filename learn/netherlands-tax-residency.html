Netherlands Tax Residency: Does the 183-Day Rule Apply?

Dutch tax residence is assessed from your circumstances, not a universal 183-day threshold. A record of your days can help explain where you live without deciding your status. Check your own dates in the calculator below.

Last verified: 19 November 2026

There is no universal 183-day allowance. Assess residence from the facts and use a day log as supporting evidence.

Where you live is a factual question

The Dutch Tax Administration considers your permanent home or place of residence and the circumstances surrounding it. Its examples include where you spend most of your time, where your partner and family live, where you work, insurance, medical care, associations and your children’s education. No single annual day total replaces that assessment.

Why 183 days still appears in tax discussions

Tax residence and the treatment of employment income under a tax treaty are separate questions. A treaty’s short-stay employment provision can involve a 183-day condition alongside other requirements. It does not create a general Dutch residency allowance.

What a day record helps explain

Keep arrival and departure dates alongside evidence of your homes, family arrangements and work. For example, someone with 120 recorded Dutch days and a continuing home and family there cannot conclude that the other 63 days are a safe allowance. The record is one part of the facts.

Moving abroad is more than stopping the counter

A move, registration change or long trip may alter the facts, but a day log cannot decide which country is your tax residence. If two countries claim residence, any applicable treaty requires a separate analysis. Record the dates accurately without treating a personal target as a legal boundary.

Two people, the same 90 days

Illustrative comparison: Alex spends 90 recorded days in the Netherlands while retaining a family home there, with a partner and children still living in it. Sam also records 90 days, spread across temporary visits while maintaining a home, family life and work abroad.

The counter returns 90 for both. That does not mean their residence position is the same, and the example does not classify either person. It shows why the facts surrounding the dates are needed.

Turn a day log into a useful residence timeline

Alongside the travel dates, note when a home became available or ceased to be available, when your household moved, and when your work pattern changed. Keep references to the documents supporting those dates. These notes help explain what a period of presence means rather than merely proving that a border was crossed.

A calendar-year view is convenient for organising the record. It does not imply that your personal connections reset on 1 January. Keep the wider chronology when a move or change of circumstances straddles the end of a year.

What a personal target can and cannot do

You might choose a target of 60 days near family, or a personal cap of 30 days for a particular travel plan. Those are valid uses of a counter. Reaching the target says that your chosen number has been reached; it says nothing by itself about Dutch tax residence. For an unqualified annual record, leave the calculator on Just count.

When seeking advice, bring the timeline and ask which connections determine your position. Asking only how many days you have left assumes a legal allowance that the domestic residence test does not provide.

Count your recorded days

Use this as a physical-presence record. It does not assess the residence tests described above. Planned stays you enter are included; an optional limit or target is your personal choice.

FAQ

Does staying below 183 days make me non-resident?

No. Dutch residence depends on the relevant facts and personal circumstances, not that universal threshold.

What limit should a personal day counter use?

None is needed for a record. If you set your own travel goal, it is a personal choice rather than a Dutch tax-residence limit.

Why keep the record at all?

It helps document where you actually spent time and supports a discussion with an adviser alongside your other ties.
