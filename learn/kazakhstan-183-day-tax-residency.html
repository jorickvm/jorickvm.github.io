Kazakhstan’s 183-Day Tax Residency Rule

Kazakhstan counts 183 days in any 12 consecutive months ending in the tax year, including travel days. Its separate vital-interests test has specific conditions.

Last verified: October 2026

You meet Kazakhstan’s ordinary presence test at 183 days in any 12 consecutive months ending in the tax year. Arrival and departure days count. You can also be resident below 183 days if you meet Kazakhstan’s centre-of-vital-interests test, which combines residence status, family and housing conditions.

Threshold
183 calendar days or more
Counting window
Any 12 consecutive months ending in the tax year
Travel days
Arrival and departure both count
Alternative route
The statutory centre-of-vital-interests test
Legal basis
2026 Tax Code, Article 222

The rule

Article 222 of the Tax Code in force from 1 January 2026 defines an individual resident. Its ordinary presence route uses a rolling 12-month period, not just January through December. Exactly 183 days is enough.

For a particular tax year, check the 12-month periods that end within that year. A period may include visits from the preceding year, and the visits do not have to be continuous.

How to count it

  1. List each stay in Kazakhstan.
  2. Include both the arrival and departure dates, counting a shared date only once.
  3. Total the dates in each relevant 12-month period.
  4. Compare the total with 183 days.

Example. You have 100 counted days late in one year and 83 early in the next, all within the same 12-month period ending in the second year. The combined 183 days meet the presence test even though neither calendar-year subtotal does.

Beyond the day count

Article 222(3) treats your centre of vital interests as being in Kazakhstan when the following conditions are met together:

  • You have Kazakh citizenship or qualifying residence permission or status.
  • Your spouse and/or close relatives live in Kazakhstan, if you have them.
  • Housing in Kazakhstan is available at any time for you or the relevant family members, owned or otherwise held by you or those family members.

This is a defined legal test, not simply a judgment that you have stronger personal or economic ties to Kazakhstan. The Code also provides a separate 90-day route for qualifying AIFC investment residents and special residence rules for certain Kazakh citizens abroad.

FAQ

Is Kazakhstan’s count based on the calendar year?

No. It uses any 12 consecutive months ending in the relevant tax year, so days in the preceding year can contribute.

Do both travel days count?

Yes. Article 222 expressly includes arrival and departure days.

Can I become resident with fewer than 183 days?

Yes. The statutory centre-of-vital-interests test can apply, and separate rules cover qualifying AIFC investment residents and certain citizens abroad.

Official sources

Article 222 of Kazakhstan’s 2026 Tax Code (official Russian-language Word document), published by the Kyzylorda State Revenue Department on its government document page. Paragraph 2 defines presence, paragraph 3 the centre of vital interests.
