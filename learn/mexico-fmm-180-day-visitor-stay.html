Mexico's 180-Day Visitor Stay: How Many Days You Actually Get

Visitors can stay up to 180 days in Mexico, but 180 is a ceiling, not a promise. Here is who decides your days, where the number is written, and how to count them.

Last verified: October 2026

In short: a visitor without permission to do paid work can stay in Mexico for up to 180 days per entry. The immigration officer decides how many days you get, anywhere from 1 to 180, and records the number with your entry stamp. The days run from the date of entry. A visitor stay cannot be extended, and you cannot switch to residence from inside Mexico. At airports the paper FMM form is gone; by land, travel beyond the border zone still needs an FMM.

Stay
Up to 180 days per entry, as granted at entry
Who
Visitors without permission to do paid work: tourism, business, study or research without pay
Your record
The entry stamp, with the date and the number of days; the FMM for land entries beyond the border zone
Counting from
The date of the entry stamp
Extension
Not possible as a visitor
Legal basis
Ley de Migración, article 52, section I

The rule

Mexico's visitor status without permission to do paid work, the status most tourists and business visitors enter on, allows an uninterrupted stay of no more than 180 days, counted from the date of entry. Three points decide most cases:

  • 180 days is the maximum. The officer at the border or airport decides how many days to grant, from 1 to 180.
  • The stamp is the record. The officer stamps your passport with the date of entry and the number of days you may stay. Check it before you leave the counter, because the number on the stamp is the one that applies.
  • It is one stay. The status covers a single entry, and it cannot be extended or changed to temporary residence from inside Mexico.

The FMM, the stamp and land borders

The Forma Migratoria Múltiple (FMM) used to be a paper card filled in on the plane. At airports it is no longer completed: the entry stamp records your days instead, and an electronic FMM can be downloaded afterwards if a formality needs one. By land, a short visit within the border zone needs no FMM. To travel further into Mexico, you need an FMM, which carries a fee, is valid for one entry and lasts at most 180 calendar days from the stamp. Paying for it does not guarantee entry.

How to count it

Count from the date on the entry stamp, with that day as day one, and stop at the number of days written on it.

Granted 180 days. You enter on 10 January 2027 and the stamp shows 180 days. Day one is 10 January, so day 180 is 8 July 2027.

Granted 30 days. On the same date, a stamp showing 30 days makes your last day 8 February 2027. There is no way to top it up to 180 from inside Mexico.

No published rule limits how many visitor stays you can have in a year, or how close together they can be. Each entry is a new decision by the officer, who can grant fewer days at any entry.

Beyond the day count

Staying past the days on your stamp breaks Mexican immigration law and can lead to detention at a migration station and removal from Mexico. Paid work for a Mexican employer or client needs a different status, applied for at a consulate before travel. If you need a longer stay, a temporary resident visa is the route, and it starts outside Mexico. For the general picture, see overstaying a visa or stay limit.

FAQ

Can I stay 180 days in Mexico as a tourist?

Up to 180 days, if the officer grants them. The number of days you may stay is decided at entry and recorded with your entry stamp, and it can be anything from 1 to 180.

Can I extend my tourist stay in Mexico?

No. A visitor stay cannot be extended, and you cannot change to temporary residence while you are in Mexico as a tourist. You have to leave by the last day on your stamp.

Do I still need an FMM to enter Mexico?

Not when you fly in: the entry stamp records your days. By land, you need an FMM to travel beyond the border zone, and it is valid for one entry of at most 180 days.

Official sources

Mexico's National Migration Institute describes the electronic FMM for land entries, valid for one entry of up to 180 calendar days from the stamp (in Spanish). The Mexican government's visitor visa page sets out the 180-day visitor status without permission to do paid work (in Spanish). Spain's Ministry of Foreign Affairs travel advice for Mexico explains that the officer sets 1 to 180 days at entry, records them with the entry stamp, and that a tourist cannot extend or change status in Mexico (in Spanish).
