EES and ETIAS: What They Mean for Your Schengen Days

Two EU border systems, two different jobs, and one thing neither of them changes: the 90/180 allowance itself. Check your own dates in the calculator.

Last verified: 11 November 2026

Quick answer: EES records border entries and exits digitally; ETIAS is a separate pre-travel authorisation for eligible visa-exempt travelers. EES has been fully operational since 10 April 2026. ETIAS is not operating and applications are not open as of 11 November 2026. Neither gives you extra Schengen days.

EES vs ETIAS

EES

Purpose
Digital entry and exit records at external borders.
Action
Complete the border process; no advance authorisation application.
Cost
No EES registration fee.
Status
Fully operational since 10 April 2026.

ETIAS

Purpose
Pre-travel authorisation for eligible visa-exempt travelers.
Action
Apply before travel once the system opens.
Announced fee
EUR 20, with exemptions.
Status
Not operating; no applications being collected.

What EES records

EES stores travel-document details, biometric data and the dates and places of entries and exits for covered non-EU travelers on short stays. It also records refusals of entry. It replaces passport stamping for those stays and supports automatic overstay detection.

Not every traveler is covered. Residence permits, long-stay visas, nationality and family-member status can affect the rules. Use the official EES guidance for your situation.

Can you check your EES remaining days?

Yes. The EU provides an EES online tool to check your remaining authorised stay. You can also ask passport-control officers for that information.

Keep your own dates as well, especially for planning future trips, checking errors or organizing travel history for other purposes. The official short-stay calculator has a planning mode for proposed stays.

Do you need to apply for ETIAS now?

No. The official ETIAS site says it is not yet operating and is not collecting applications. The EU says it will announce the specific start date several months before launch. Check that site before travel rather than relying on an old projected launch date.

Once available, ETIAS will cover eligible visa-exempt travelers visiting 30 European countries. The announced application fee is EUR 20, with exemptions. Authorisation is normally valid for up to 3 years or until the passport expires, whichever comes first. It is not a visa or a residence permit and does not guarantee admission.

The ETIAS country list and the Schengen list are different: Cyprus is included in ETIAS, but its stay allowance is counted separately from Schengen.

Does EES or ETIAS reset the 90/180 rule?

No. For ordinary Schengen short stays, the allowance remains 90 days in any rolling 180-day period, shared across the area. Entry and exit days count. A digital border record or a valid travel authorisation does not add days.

FAQ

Does EES change the 90 days in 180 rule?

No. EES changes how covered border movements are recorded. The ordinary Schengen short-stay allowance remains 90 days in any rolling 180-day period.

Do I need an ETIAS right now?

No. As of 11 November 2026, ETIAS is not operating and applications are not open. Check the official EU website before travel for the announced start date.

Will my passport still be stamped?

For short stays covered by EES, a digital entry and exit record replaces passport stamping. Travelers outside EES follow the rules applicable to their own status.

Official sources
