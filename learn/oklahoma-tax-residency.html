Oklahoma's Seven-Month Residency Presumption

Spending more than seven months of a tax year in Oklahoma, in total, presumes you are an Oklahoma resident. The presumption can be rebutted. Here is how the count works.

Last verified: November 2026

In short: Oklahoma residents are people domiciled in Oklahoma. Anyone else who spends, in the aggregate, more than seven months of the taxable year in the state is presumed to be a resident, in the absence of proof to the contrary. Oklahoma does not say how many days make seven months, so the cautious reading treats anything past 210 days as more than seven months.

The rule

Oklahoma defines a resident as a person domiciled in the state, and adds two presumptions based on time:

  • More than seven months. Someone who spends, in the aggregate, more than seven months of the taxable year in Oklahoma is presumed to be a resident.
  • Less than seven months. Someone who resides in Oklahoma for less than seven months of the year is presumed to be a part-year resident.

Both presumptions give way to proof to the contrary, and no home in Oklahoma is needed for either.

How to count it

  1. Count every day you spent in Oklahoma, including days you were there for only part of the day.
  2. Add the days across the taxable year, 1 January to 31 December for most people. They do not need to be consecutive.
  3. Oklahoma states the rule in months, not days. If your total is past 210 days, treat it as more than seven months.

Example. You are domiciled in Texas and work at an Oklahoma City office from 1 January to 31 July, driving home most weekends. Your days in Oklahoma add up to 160.

That is well under seven months, so the resident presumption does not arise. Had you stayed through the weekends as well, the same seven months would have come to 212 days, past the cautious line.

Beyond the day count

The presumptions decide who carries the burden of proof, not the outcome. A person domiciled in Oklahoma is a resident at any day count, and someone clearly domiciled elsewhere can rebut the presumption even after more than seven months in the state.

FAQ

How long can you stay in Oklahoma without being presumed a resident?

Up to seven months of the taxable year, in total. Oklahoma does not convert the months into days, so staying at 210 days or fewer is the cautious course.

Is Oklahoma's seven-month rule automatic?

No. It is a presumption that applies in the absence of proof to the contrary, such as a domicile clearly elsewhere.

What if you spend less than seven months in Oklahoma?

You are presumed to be a part-year resident rather than a full-year one, again unless the facts show otherwise.

Official source

68 O.S. § 2353, the Oklahoma Income Tax Act's definitions, in the Oklahoma Legislature's Title 68 statutes.
