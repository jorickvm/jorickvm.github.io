Alabama's Seven-Month Residency Presumption

A permanent place of abode in Alabama, or more than seven months in the state in a tax year, presumes you are an Alabama resident, even if your domicile is elsewhere. Here is how the count works.

Last verified: November 2026

In short: Alabama presumes you are a resident, even if you are domiciled elsewhere, if you maintain a permanent place of abode in Alabama or spend more than seven months of the taxable year in the state, in total. Either one is enough. Alabama does not say how many days make seven months, so the cautious reading treats anything past 210 days as more than seven months.

The rule

Alabama residents are people domiciled in Alabama. For people domiciled elsewhere, Alabama's residency rule adds a presumption with two separate triggers:

  • A permanent place of abode in Alabama. A dwelling you permanently maintain, whether or not you own it or live in it yourself. Keeping one is enough on its own.
  • More than seven months. Spending, in total, more than seven months of the taxable year in Alabama, whether or not consecutive, is also enough on its own.

Either one presumes you are a resident, taxable on your net income from within and outside Alabama, and expected to file a resident return. The presumption can be rebutted.

How to count it

  1. Count every day you spent in Alabama, including days you were there for only part of the day.
  2. Add the days across the taxable year, 1 January to 31 December for most people. They do not need to be consecutive.
  3. Alabama states the rule in months, not days. If your total is past 210 days, treat it as more than seven months.
  4. Separately, check whether you keep a permanent place of abode in Alabama: that alone triggers the presumption.

Example. You are domiciled in Illinois and spend the cooler months in a rented condominium in Gulf Shores, from 1 January to 30 April and again from 1 November to 31 December. Counting both travel days of each stay, that is 181 days in Alabama.

That is under seven months. But if the condominium counts as a permanent place of abode in Alabama, the presumption arises on that ground alone, whatever your day count.

Beyond the day count

The presumption only decides where the burden lies. Alabama can ask anyone claiming a domicile outside the state for a statement supporting the claim. Domicile itself is a separate test: an Alabama domiciliary is a resident at any day count.

FAQ

How long can you stay in Alabama without being presumed a resident?

Up to seven months of the taxable year, in total, if you keep no permanent place of abode there. Alabama does not convert the months into days, so staying at 210 days or fewer is the cautious course.

Does a home in Alabama alone make you a presumed resident?

Yes. Keeping a permanent place of abode in Alabama triggers the presumption on its own, whatever your day count.

Can the Alabama presumption be rebutted?

Yes. It is a presumption, not a final rule. You overcome it by showing your domicile is outside Alabama, which the Department may ask you to support with a statement.

Official source

Alabama Department of Revenue Rule 810-3-2-.01, Residence of Individuals, in the Alabama Administrative Code.
