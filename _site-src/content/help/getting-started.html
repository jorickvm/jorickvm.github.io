AtlasDays / Help Center

Getting Started

AtlasDays records which country you were in on which day, then counts those days against the limits that matter to you. Trips live in the Timeline, the Dashboard shows what they add up to, and a tracker watches one specific rule.

What AtlasDays is for

Questions with a consequence attached: how many Schengen 90/180 days are still available, whether you are approaching a 183-day residency threshold, how long you have really been out of your home country this year.

AtlasDays keeps the record and counts the days. Interpreting an immigration or tax rule is still your responsibility.

Fill your Timeline without typing

Photos cover the past. Auto-Detect covers what comes next.

Open Settings → Import → Photos and tap Find Trips in Photos. Photo Import reads geotagged photo metadata on the device and proposes trips for review, and nothing is saved until you confirm. Use CSV Import instead when your history is already in a spreadsheet or a booking record, or Flighty Import when your Flighty flight history covers most of it. Import Trips compares the sources.

Then turn on Auto-Detect Trips in Settings → Tracking, so new travel is added to the Timeline automatically rather than entered by hand. Review the trip there or from its notification; you can edit or delete it at any time.

The Timeline holds the record

Every trip, in order, with the dates you gave it. Tap a trip to edit it, or tap + to add one by hand.

Older history does not need precise dates: a trip can be saved as Year Only when you only know the year, which keeps the country on your map without inventing days. Anything feeding a live limit needs its real dates.

The Dashboard shows what it adds up to

Days abroad, countries and continents visited, the world map, and your trackers. The period control on the Statistics card switches between a single year, a range, or all time, so the same record answers different questions.

Set your Home Country. Onboarding does not ask for it, and it starts as Not set. Choose it in Settings → Tracking → Home Country so that Days Abroad knows which days are away. It also affects how the Add Tracker list is ordered. Choose No fixed residence to count every recorded day.

Add your first tracker

A tracker is the reason to use AtlasDays rather than a travel diary. On the Dashboard, tap + to open Add a Tracker. Pick a preset such as the Schengen 90/180 rule or a 183-day tax residency threshold, or search by country name. It then shows used days, remaining days, and how close you are. Create a Tracker covers the options.

A tracker is only as good as the trips behind it. Treat its total as provisional while the relevant trips are still approximate. Tracking one limit is free; more trackers and some rule types need Pro.

If the first totals look wrong

Check the selected Dashboard period, your Home Country setting, any open-ended trip still counting toward today, Transit trips, and whether a trip that matters was saved as Year Only.
