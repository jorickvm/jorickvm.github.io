Add, Edit, or Delete a Trip

The Timeline is the source record behind the Dashboard, map, trackers, exports, and widgets. Edit the trip there when any of those views need different input.

Add a trip

  1. Open Timeline and tap +, or tap + on the Dashboard and choose Add Trip. Before your first trip the Timeline has no +: tap Add by Hand on the Add your first trip card instead.
  2. Tap Select country and choose one. For a US trip, you can add a state under State (optional) when you want state-level tracking (Pro).
  3. Tap Start date and End date and set both. For a stay that has not ended, tap Open-ended on the end-date page. How Trip Dates Work covers Open-ended, Year Only, and Transit.
  4. Optionally choose a purpose or add Notes (optional), then tap Save.

Add several past trips in a row

To write down a run of trips from memory, open Settings → Import → Add by Hand. Add Trips shows one card per trip, oldest at the top. Fill in a card, then tap + above the cards for an earlier trip or below them for a later one. Where days between two trips are not covered, Add Trip in Between appears.

Each card is saved as soon as its country and dates are complete, and shows Saved. Cards that repeat or overlap a trip you already have are marked, as in an import. Tap Done when you have finished. Because the trips are already saved, Cancel asks first: Discard deletes the trips you entered on this screen, and Keep Editing keeps them.

Open a trip

Tap a trip in the Timeline list, or its bar in the calendar, to open its page. The page shows the dates and day count, the trackers the trip counts toward, its photos, and a note you can type straight into. Tap a tracker to open it.

The bar at the bottom of the page names the trips before and after this one. Tap it, or pull it sideways, to move to the Earlier or Later trip without going back to the list.

Edit a trip

On the trip's page, tap the edit button at the top right. Or touch and hold the trip in the list and choose Edit. Change the country, state, dates, or purposes, then tap Save. Related totals refresh from the edited record.

Below Delete Trip, the form says how the trip was created and when: for example Imported from Flighty, Auto-detected, or Added manually, followed by the date and time. Trips that were in AtlasDays before version 1.6.1 show no such line.

Delete a trip

Touch and hold the trip in the list and choose Delete. Or open the trip, tap the edit button, and tap Delete Trip. AtlasDays always asks you to confirm, and a deletion cannot be undone, so restore a trip deleted by mistake by adding it again.

Switch between List and Calendar

On iPhone, tap the arrow beside ••• at the top left of the Timeline, or swipe sideways across it. The list is fastest for reviewing trips in order. The calendar makes gaps and trips spanning weeks easier to see, and shows only trips with exact dates. Tap a trip's bar to open it, or tap elsewhere in a day to see its trips in a sheet, and use + there to add a trip on that date. Both views edit the same records, and each keeps its place when you switch back to it.

On iPad and an opened iPhone Duo, the list and the calendar sit side by side instead. The button with two arrows beside ••• swaps them, and both keep their place.

To search, jump to a year or month, or change sorting and colors, see Search and Customize the Timeline.

United States stays

A United States trip without a state still contributes to country-level US totals. With Pro, you can add the state, which shows as a flag on the trip card. Consecutive US trips appear as one United States card. Tap it to see each state on its own card, or tap + on that page to add another state to the stay. Add the state when you need a state tracker or the US states statistic.

If a trip cannot be saved

Save stays unavailable until a country and dates are set: a start and an end date (or Open-ended), or a year for Year Only. Check that the end date is not before the start date. If the message Duplicate Trip appears, that exact trip already exists. Overlaps do not block saving; see Resolve Overlapping Trips.
