AtlasDays on iPad

AtlasDays uses the extra iPad space to set the Dashboard in two columns, show the Timeline list and calendar side by side, open a tracker's detail in two panes, and split Settings into a sidebar and a detail pane. Trips, trackers, and counting rules are the same as on iPhone.

Dashboard

On a wide iPad screen, the Dashboard sets its cards in two columns: the statistics and the map on the left, your trackers on the right. With the iPad upright, the map spans both columns. Reorder in the ••• menu shows the two columns as two groups, so you can order the cards within each. The selected period, visit rules, and day totals are identical to iPhone.

Tracker details

Tap a tracker card and its detail opens in two panes: the tracker and its chart on the left, and the trips that count, with their sort order, on the right. On a narrower screen, such as iPad mini upright, and at the largest text sizes, the detail is one column.

Timeline

The Timeline shows the trip list and the calendar together, so there is no list and calendar switch to tap. The list starts on the left. To put it on the other side, tap the button with two arrows beside •••; the panes slide across and keep their width and their place. In a narrow window, such as a small Stage Manager window, the Timeline falls back to one view at a time, as on iPhone.

Settings

Settings uses a sidebar and a detail pane. Choose Import, Export, Appearance, Language, iCloud Sync, or another destination on the left, and work in the pane on the right. Import and Export open their own options inside that pane, and Widgets opens there as a page rather than a sheet.

Sync between iPhone and iPad

Turn on iCloud Sync on each device that uses the same Apple Account when you want trips and trackers to stay aligned. To move data to a device that does not use iCloud, restore an AtlasDays Backup there. The same two-pane layouts appear on an opened iPhone Duo.

If a pane looks empty or the layout is one column

Choose a destination in the Settings sidebar. If the layout looks cramped, rotate the iPad or widen the window: a narrow Stage Manager window uses the compact layout, and the Dashboard and tracker details need more width for two columns than the Timeline and Settings need for two panes.
