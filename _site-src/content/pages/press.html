Descriptions

Use these as they are, or edit them to fit.

Short

AtlasDays is a private iPhone app that counts your days in each country against visa limits and tax-residency thresholds, such as the Schengen 90/180 rule.

Longer

AtlasDays answers a question every frequent traveler is eventually asked: where were you, and for how many days? It logs trips by country and by US state, and counts the days against rules like the Schengen 90/180 rule, 183-day tax-residency tests and the US Substantial Presence Test. Planned trips are counted too, so a tracker shows before departure whether a trip still fits.

Trips can be added by hand, detected automatically when you cross a border, or rebuilt from the photos already in your library, from Flighty, or from a spreadsheet. AtlasDays has no account and no ads, and the trips stay on the iPhone or in the user's own iCloud. It is made by Jorick van Meijeren, an independent developer in Tbilisi.

Key features

  • Trackers for real rules. Presets for Schengen 90/180, single-entry visas, tax residency and citizenship absences, or a custom limit over any rolling window.
  • Forecasts for planned trips. A tracker counts upcoming trips, so it shows in advance whether a plan stays within the limit.
  • Trips from Photos. Photo Import scans the dates and locations in the photo library on the device and suggests past trips to confirm.
  • Auto-Detect. With location access, AtlasDays adds a trip when the iPhone enters a new country. Every trip can be edited or deleted afterwards.
  • A record for paperwork. Spreadsheet and PDF exports of the trip history, for visa applications and tax filings.
  • The map and the globe. Every country visited, on a world map and a 3D globe.

Background

Jorick van Meijeren left the Netherlands in 2019 and has since lived in Spain, Indonesia, Portugal, El Salvador and Georgia. AtlasDays started as his own tool when he moved to Tbilisi and needed to count days toward Georgia's tax residency: 183 days in any rolling 365-day window. With a few trips on the calendar, that count gets hard to keep in your head or in a spreadsheet, and the visa and residency tools he tried either missed his case or wanted his whole travel history on their servers.

He also wanted a better travel map. He had used Been for years to keep track of the countries he had visited, but it records visits by month rather than by date, which isn't precise enough when the day count matters.
