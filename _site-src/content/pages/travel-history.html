Get AtlasDays How to rebuild a history

The form wants ten years. You have a photo library.

Visa and citizenship applications ask for every trip, sometimes over a decade. The evidence is passport stamps, old boarding passes, half-remembered summers, and a camera roll.

Most tools make you choose between precise and complete, so people guess. An invented date is worse than an honest gap: it is the one an officer can disprove.

Import first, type last

Geotagged photos, a CSV, or a Flighty flight history become draft trips. Overlaps, gaps, incomplete entries, and merge candidates are flagged for you to resolve, and nothing is written to your record until you approve it.

Exact where it counts, approximate where it does not

Record 2019 as a year, or a country with no dates at all, while keeping exact dates for the trips a limit actually depends on. Approximate history stays useful without turning into fake precision, and the app never quietly promotes a guess into a fact.

Export in the shape the form wants

A clean PDF or spreadsheet of trips, days, and countries, ready to attach to an application instead of being retyped into one. The same record that drives your day counts is the one you hand over.

What this does not do

An import is a starting point, not a source of truth. Photo metadata can be wrong, flight data misses overland crossings, and only you know which trips are missing. AtlasDays makes the record easy to build and easy to correct. It cannot verify it for you.
