Malaysia's 182-Day Tax Residency Rule

Malaysia’s main residence test starts at 182 days in a calendar year. Linked stays and earlier or later years can establish residence with fewer days.

Last verified: 12 November 2026

In short: you become a Malaysian tax resident if you are in Malaysia for 182 days or more in a calendar year (note: 182, not 183). Every part of a day counts as a whole day, so arrival and departure days are both included, and the days need not be consecutive. Linked-period and multi-year tests are secondary routes in.

The rule

Malaysia treats you as a tax resident for a year if you are in the country for 182 days or more during that calendar year. Section 7(1)(a) of the Income Tax Act 1967 puts it as a period or periods amounting in all to one hundred and eighty-two days or more. Three points decide most real cases:

  • It is 182, not 183. Malaysia's threshold is at least 182 days, one day fewer than a 183-day threshold. Reach 182 and you are resident; 181 or fewer and the primary test is not met.
  • Part of a day is a whole day. Under Section 7(1A) you are deemed present for a day if you are in Malaysia for any part of it, so both your arrival day and your departure day count in full.
  • Days need not be consecutive. The count adds up separate visits across the calendar year; a day counts whether or not it forms part of a continuous stay.

How to count it

  1. List every Malaysia trip with arrival and departure dates.
  2. Count each day you were present, including arrival and departure days, since any part of a day counts as a whole day.
  3. Total the days that fall inside one calendar year (1 January to 31 December), keeping each year separate.
  4. If any calendar year reaches 182 days or more, the primary day-count test is met and you are resident for that year.

Example. In one calendar year you make three trips to Malaysia: 70 days in the spring, 60 days in the summer, and 52 days in the autumn.

No single trip is long, but the days need not be consecutive. Together they total 182 days in the same calendar year, so you meet the threshold and are a Malaysian tax resident for that year.

Can you be resident with fewer than 182 days?

Yes. Section 7 has several other routes. A count below 182 only rules out the main test in paragraph 7(1)(a).

  • Linked periods, paragraph 7(1)(b). A shorter stay can qualify if linked to at least 182 consecutive days in the immediately preceding or following year. Certain temporary absences can form part of the linked periods when you are in Malaysia immediately before and after the absence: absences connected with your Malaysian service, your own or an immediate family member’s ill health, and social visits totalling no more than 14 days.
  • 90-day history test, paragraph 7(1)(c). You spend at least 90 days in the current year and, in any three of the four preceding years, were either resident or present for at least 90 days. The qualifying earlier years can use a combination of those conditions.
  • Residence in surrounding years, paragraph 7(1)(d). You are resident in the three immediately preceding years and the following year. This can establish residence even with no Malaysian presence in the current year.

A separate rule in subsection 7(1B) covers certain Malaysian citizens employed in Malaysian public service or a statutory authority who are abroad for their employment or fully employer-funded study. Citizenship alone does not establish residence.

If you are resident in two countries, check the applicable tax treaty. Its residence tie-breaker determines residence for treaty purposes; it does not simply erase the domestic Malaysian classification.

FAQ

How many days can you stay in Malaysia without becoming a tax resident?

There is no universal safe number. A total of 181 days or fewer stays below the main 182-day test, but the linked-period and multi-year rules can still make you resident, sometimes with very few days or none in that year.

Is it 182 or 183 days in Malaysia?

It is 182 days, unusually one day fewer than a 183-day threshold.

Do arrival and departure days count?

Yes. Any part of a day counts as a whole day, so both your arrival day and your departure day are included.
