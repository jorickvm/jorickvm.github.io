Indiana's 183-Day Residency Rule

A permanent place of residence in Indiana plus more than 183 days in the state in a tax year makes you an Indiana resident, even if your domicile is elsewhere. Here is how the count works.

Last verified: November 2026

In short: Indiana treats you as a resident if you maintain a permanent place of residence in Indiana and spend more than 183 days of the taxable year in the state. Both conditions must be met, and the day condition is met on the 184th day. Indiana does not define a day for this test, so the cautious count includes every day you spend any part of in the state.

The rule

Indiana has two ways to be a resident: by domicile, the place you intend to live permanently and return to whenever you leave, or by the 183-day test, which applies even if you are domiciled elsewhere. The 183-day test has two conditions that must both be met:

  • A permanent place of residence. A building or structure you maintain where you can live, suitable for year-round use, whether you own it or not. A residence kept mainly to earn an educational degree does not count.
  • More than 183 days. You spend more than 183 days of the taxable year in Indiana, so 184 days or more.

Meet both and Indiana treats you as a resident for the year.

How to count it

  1. Confirm you maintain a permanent place of residence in Indiana.
  2. Count every day you spent in Indiana, including days you were there for only part of the day.
  3. Add the days across the taxable year, 1 January to 31 December for most people. They do not need to be consecutive.
  4. If the total reaches 184 and you keep that residence, Indiana treats you as a resident for that year.

Example. You are domiciled in Florida and keep a condominium in Indianapolis that you use from spring to autumn, arriving on 1 April and leaving on 1 October. Counting both travel days, that is 184 days in Indiana.

With a permanent place of residence and more than 183 days, Indiana treats you as a resident for the year. Leaving on 30 September instead keeps you at 183, which is not more than 183.

Beyond the day count

Indiana domicile is hard to lose. Once you are domiciled in Indiana, you keep that status until you voluntarily take positive action to become domiciled in another state or country and give up the rights and privileges of Indiana residency. Moving away with definite plans to leave the new place again does not establish a new domicile. The 183-day test works the other way round: it catches people whose domicile is elsewhere but who keep a year-round home in Indiana.

FAQ

How many days can you spend in Indiana without becoming a resident?

Up to 183 days in the taxable year. The test needs more than 183 days together with a permanent place of residence in Indiana.

What counts as a permanent place of residence in Indiana?

A building or structure you maintain where you can live, suitable for year-round use, whether you own it or rent it. A residence kept mainly to earn a degree does not count.

Do days alone make you an Indiana resident?

No. Without a permanent place of residence in Indiana the 183-day test does not apply, though domicile can still make you a resident.
