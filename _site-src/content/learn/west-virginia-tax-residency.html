West Virginia's 183-Day Residency Rule

More than 183 days in West Virginia in a tax year can make you a West Virginia resident, even if you are domiciled in another state. Any part of a day counts. Here is how the count works.

Last verified: November 2026

In short: West Virginia's code treats you as a resident if you are not domiciled there but maintain a permanent place of abode in the state and spend more than 183 days of the taxable year there. The Tax Division's return instructions put it more simply: a physical presence of more than 183 days makes you a resident, even if another state also treats you as one. Any part of a calendar day in the state counts as a day.

The rule

West Virginia residents are people domiciled in the state, and people domiciled elsewhere who meet the 183-day test. The code sets two conditions for that test:

  • A permanent place of abode in West Virginia. A home you maintain in the state.
  • More than 183 days. You spend, in total, more than 183 days of the taxable year in West Virginia, so 184 days or more.

The Tax Division's instructions describe a resident as someone who maintains a physical presence in West Virginia for more than 183 days of the taxable year, without mentioning the home. If you are near the line, the day count is what decides it, so count it as if no home were needed.

How to count it

  1. Count every day you were in West Virginia for any part of the day. Under the Tax Division's rules, any part of a calendar day counts.
  2. Add the days across the taxable year, 1 January to 31 December for most people. They do not need to be consecutive.
  3. If the total reaches 184, West Virginia can treat you as a resident for that year.

Example. You are domiciled in Virginia and keep a house near Harpers Ferry, where you spend long weekends all year and most of the summer. Across the year you are in West Virginia on parts of 186 days.

Because any part of a day counts, all 186 days count. With more than 183 days, West Virginia treats you as a resident for the year.

Beyond the day count

West Virginia also has a 30-day rule for its own domiciliaries. Someone domiciled in West Virginia is a nonresident for the year if they maintain no permanent place of abode in the state, maintain one elsewhere, and spend no more than 30 days in West Virginia; the Tax Division's instructions say fewer than 30. Residents of Kentucky, Maryland, Ohio, Pennsylvania and Virginia whose only West Virginia income is wages are taxed as special nonresidents, but the Tax Division treats Pennsylvania and Virginia domiciliaries as West Virginia residents once they are physically present for more than 183 days.

FAQ

How many days can you spend in West Virginia without becoming a resident?

Up to 183 days in the taxable year, if you are domiciled elsewhere. At 184 days West Virginia can treat you as a resident.

What counts as a day in West Virginia?

Any part of a calendar day spent in the state counts as a day.

What is West Virginia's 30-day rule?

A West Virginia domiciliary is a nonresident for the year if they keep no permanent place of abode in the state, keep one elsewhere, and spend no more than 30 days in West Virginia.
