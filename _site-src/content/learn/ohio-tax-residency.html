Ohio's 212 Contact-Period Residency Rule

Ohio counts contact periods, not simply hotel nights. At most 212 is only one condition for its nonresident safe harbor; your homes, identification, benefits and annual statement also matter.

Last verified: 12 November 2026

In short: a contact period combines time in Ohio on two consecutive days with being away overnight from your home outside Ohio. The night need not be in Ohio. Having 212 or fewer contact periods is only one of five conditions for an irrebuttable nonresident presumption, and a timely statement is also required. If you have an Ohio abode and do not qualify for that protection, Ohio can presume you are resident even below 213 contact periods. At 213 or more, the evidence needed to rebut the presumption is stronger.

The nonresident safe harbor

The special presumption under §5747.24(B) requires all five conditions:

  • Contact periods: no more than 212 during the taxable year.
  • Outside abode: at least one qualifying home outside Ohio for the entire year, with no federal depreciation deduction claimed on it.
  • Ohio identification: no valid Ohio driver's license or state ID during the year. Surrendering it must happen before the year starts.
  • Property-tax reductions: no Ohio homestead exemption or owner-occupancy tax reduction covered by the rule.
  • Tuition: no Ohio residence-based in-state tuition for the individual claiming the presumption.

You must also claim the Ohio Nonresident Statement on time, and the safe harbor does not apply to a year in which you change domicile into or out of Ohio. When its conditions are truthfully satisfied, the nonresident presumption is irrebuttable. A false statement does not provide that protection.

How to count contact periods

  1. Identify each overnight away from your abode outside Ohio.
  2. Check whether you spent any part of both consecutive days in Ohio while away from that abode.
  3. Count one contact period for each qualifying overnight. A day trip followed by a night at your outside home does not qualify.
  4. Add the contact periods for the taxable year. Then check the other safe-harbor conditions and the statement requirement separately.

Example. You keep your home in Kentucky, spend part of Monday in Ohio, sleep in a Kentucky hotel away from that home, then return to Ohio on Tuesday. That is one Ohio contact period even though you did not sleep in Ohio. If you go back to your own Kentucky home on Monday night, that pair of visits does not create a contact period.

What happens outside the safe harbor?

Ohio's underlying residence question is domicile, not a simple night limit. If you have an abode in Ohio at any time in the taxable year and are not protected by the safe harbor, §5747.24(C) and (D) create resident presumptions. Below 213 contact periods, rebuttal requires a preponderance of the evidence. At 213 or more, it requires clear and convincing evidence. Changing domicile during the year has separate part-year treatment.

Keep records of the Ohio portions of each day and where you stayed overnight. Counting nights physically spent in Ohio alone can miss legal contact periods.

Claim the statement for the right tax year

The 2025 instructions claim the presumption through the Ohio Nonresident Statement box on IT 10 or IT 1040. Use the form for the year you are reporting. The statement deadline is the 15th day of the tenth month after the tax year ends, normally 15 October for a calendar-year filer. That deadline does not extend the return's ordinary filing deadline.

FAQ

Does staying below 213 contact periods guarantee Ohio nonresidency?

No. At most 212 is only one safe-harbor condition. You must meet the other four conditions, claim the annual statement on time and not be a part-year resident. An Ohio abode can trigger a rebuttable resident presumption even below 213 contact periods.

Must you spend the night in Ohio to have a contact period?

No. You must be away overnight from your abode outside Ohio and spend part of two consecutive days in Ohio. An overnight in a Kentucky hotel can therefore create an Ohio contact period; returning to your own outside home does not.

What changes at 213 contact periods?

If you have an Ohio abode and are subject to the resident presumption, reaching 213 contact periods changes the rebuttal standard from a preponderance of the evidence to clear and convincing evidence. It does not turn domicile into an automatic night-count rule.
