Nebraska's 183-Day Statutory Residency Rule

Nebraska’s published guidance applies a 183-day threshold together with a permanent place of abode. The statute uses more than six months, and domicile remains a separate route.

Last verified: 12 November 2026

In short: Nebraska’s Department of Revenue guidance treats you as a resident if you maintain a permanent place of abode in the state and are present for 183 days or more during the taxable year. This applies even if your permanent home is in another state or country. Any part of a day in Nebraska counts as a full day, and Nebraska catches you at 183 days, not 184.

The rule

Nebraska has two separate ways to be a resident: by domicile (Nebraska is your true, permanent home) or by statutory residence. The 183-day rule is the statutory-residence test, and it has two conditions that must both be met:

  • A permanent place of abode. You permanently maintain a dwelling in Nebraska, whether or not you own or occupy it. The guidance includes accommodation used for vacations if suitable for human occupation, but excludes a vacation rental or dwelling occupied only temporarily.
  • 183 days or more. The statute uses more than six months; the Department’s guidance applies at least 183 days in the taxable year and counts any part of a day as a day. Unlike New York’s more-than-183 rule, that guidance reaches exactly 183.

Meeting both brings you within the Department’s statutory-residence guidance even if you are domiciled elsewhere. Residents are generally taxed on income from inside and outside Nebraska, subject to the income-tax rules and any credit for tax paid to another state.

How to count it

  1. Confirm you maintained a permanent place of abode in Nebraska during the year.
  2. Count every day on which you were present in Nebraska for any part of the day.
  3. Add the days across the whole taxable year. Days do not need to be consecutive, and time in the state counts even when you are not at your Nebraska abode.
  4. If the total reaches 183 or more and the place-of-abode condition is met, you are a statutory resident for that year.

Example. You are domiciled in Texas but keep an apartment in Omaha all year for work. Across the year you are physically in Nebraska on 185 days, many of them just for part of the day.

Because any part of a day counts, all 185 days count. With a permanent place of abode plus 183 or more days, you meet Nebraska’s published day-count test despite your Texas domicile.

Beyond the day count

The day-count test does not replace domicile, which turns on your true, fixed home and intent. Temporary or transitory absence does not end Nebraska domicile. Changing domicile into or out of Nebraska can make you a partial-year resident. Keep the residence classification separate from the filing requirements and calculation of tax on a particular income source.

FAQ

How many days can you spend in Nebraska without becoming a statutory resident?

Under the Department’s day-count guidance, fewer than 183 days stays below the presence threshold. At least 183 days plus a permanent place of abode meets it. Domicile can establish residence separately.

What counts as a day in Nebraska?

Any part of a day spent in Nebraska counts as a full day. Time in the state counts even when you are not occupying your Nebraska place of abode.

Do you need a home in Nebraska to be caught by the rule?

For the statutory test, yes. Without a permanent place of abode there is no statutory residence on days alone, though separate domicile rules can still make you a resident.
