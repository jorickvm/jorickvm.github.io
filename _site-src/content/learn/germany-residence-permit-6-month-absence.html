Germany Residence Permit: The Six-Month Absence Rule

A German residence permit, even a settlement permit, lapses if you stay abroad for more than six months without permission. Here is how the six months run, who gets twelve, and who is protected.

Last verified: October 2026

In short: a German residence title expires if you leave Germany and do not come back within six months, unless the immigration office (Ausländerbehörde) set a longer period before you went. The limit is twelve months for EU Blue Card holders and their families, and for some settlement-permit holders aged 60 or over. It also expires at once if you leave for a reason that is not temporary, such as moving abroad. People with a settlement permit after 15 years of lawful residence, and the spouses of German citizens, are protected if they meet the conditions below.

The rule

Section 51 of the Residence Act lists the ways a residence title ends. Two of them are about leaving Germany:

  • Six months away. The title expires if you leave and do not re-enter within six months, or within a longer period that the immigration office has set.
  • Leaving for good. The title expires as soon as you leave for a reason that is not temporary by nature, such as giving up your home and job to move abroad, even if you come back within six months.

Both apply to a settlement permit as well as to a temporary residence permit. The six months are calendar months, counted from the day you leave, not a number of days.

Getting a longer period before you go

If you know you will be away for more than six months, ask your immigration office to set a longer period before you leave. It should normally agree when the stay abroad is temporary and you hold a settlement permit, or when the stay abroad serves German interests. Get the decision in writing and carry it when you return.

The EU long-term residence permit (Erlaubnis zum Daueraufenthalt – EU) follows its own rule: it lapses after twelve consecutive months outside the EU, or 24 months for former Blue Card holders.

How to count it

Example. You leave Germany on 15 January 2027. Six months later is 15 July 2027, and that is the last day to be back. Counted as days, 180 days away would end a day earlier, on 14 July, which makes 180 days the safer figure to plan with.

A visit resets it. If you come back to Germany in between, the absence ends and a later departure starts a new six months, provided you have not moved abroad for good.

Beyond the day count

Long absences matter for naturalisation too, where the test is different: a stay abroad of up to six months does not interrupt habitual residence. See Germany's naturalisation absence limit. If you hold residence in another EU country as well, its own rules apply separately; see residence permit and citizenship absence rules.

FAQ

How long can I leave Germany with a residence permit?

Up to six months, unless the immigration office set a longer period before you left. EU Blue Card holders and their families have twelve months. Leaving for a reason that is not temporary ends the permit at once.

Does a settlement permit (Niederlassungserlaubnis) expire if I live abroad?

Yes, after six months away or as soon as you move abroad for good, unless you have lived in Germany lawfully for 15 years with a secured livelihood, or are married to a German citizen and living together, and there is no ground for expulsion.

Can I get permission to stay abroad longer than six months?

Yes. Ask your immigration office before you leave. It should normally set a longer period when the stay abroad is temporary and you hold a settlement permit, or when it serves German interests.
