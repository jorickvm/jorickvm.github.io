Mexico Tax Residency: Why 183 Days Is Not the Test

Mexico’s domestic residence rules focus on your home and, where you have a home abroad too, your centre of vital interests. A visitor allowance is a separate matter.

Last verified: 19 November 2026

Start with your home and centre of vital interests. A visitor allowance or a count below 183 does not establish non-residence.

Start with Article 9

Mexico’s Federal Fiscal Code treats individuals who establish their home in Mexico as resident. When they also have a home in another country, the Mexican centre-of-vital-interests test applies. The statute lists Mexican-source income exceeding half of total annual income and the main centre of professional activities in Mexico among the relevant cases.

Nationality and other circumstances matter

The code also contains a rebuttable presumption for Mexican nationals and specific provisions for government personnel. The broad statement that anyone becomes resident only after 183 days therefore misses the actual domestic test.

Do not borrow a tourist limit

Immigration permission determines whether and for how long you may remain as a visitor. Tax residence concerns your home and ties. A visitor permission or a travel count below 183 does not answer the tax question.

Use the record as evidence

Keep an annual log of visits together with information about homes and professional activities. Someone with homes in Mexico and abroad should not interpret 100 Mexican days as proof of non-residence. The log documents presence; the rest of the circumstances still need assessment.

FAQ

Is there a general Mexican 183-day residence test?

Article 9 uses home and centre-of-vital-interests criteria rather than a general 183-day threshold.

Does my tourist permission decide my tax residence?

No. Immigration status and tax residence are separate.

Can a day log still help?

Yes. It documents physical presence, but it should be considered with housing, income and professional ties.
