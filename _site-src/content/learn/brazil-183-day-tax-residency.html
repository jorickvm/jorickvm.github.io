Brazil’s 183-Day Tax Residency Rule

Brazil's tax residence test starts on day 184 within an arrival-based 12-month period. Permanent residence or local employment can make you resident sooner.

Last verified: October 2026

A temporary-visa holder becomes a Brazilian tax resident on completing 184 days in Brazil within a period of up to 12 months. Separate visits add together. Arrival with permanent residence or to work under a Brazilian employment contract can establish residence immediately.

Threshold
184 days, consecutive or not
Counting window
Up to 12 months, starting at an arrival
Resident from
The 184th day, unless an earlier route applies
Immediate residence
Arrival with permanent residence or to work under a Brazilian employment contract
Legal basis
IN SRF 208/2002, Article 2

The rule

Brazil’s threshold is more than 183 days, so the day-count route begins on day 184. The period starts at an arrival. It is not a calendar-year allowance and a short trip abroad does not erase the days already spent in Brazil.

If you do not complete 184 days in that 12-month period, Receita Federal says to count a new period from the entry following the one that started the previous count. Keep the entry dates as well as the total: the law anchors the periods to arrivals.

How to count it

  1. List your entries into and departures from Brazil.
  2. Starting with an entry, total your days of presence over the following period of up to 12 months, including separate visits.
  3. Identify the date on which that total reaches 184 days.
  4. If it does not, apply the next arrival-based period described above. Do not reset the count on 1 January.

Example. You spend 100 days in Brazil, leave, and return for another 84 days within the same arrival-based 12-month period. Assuming no earlier residence trigger, the final day of the second stay is day 184 and establishes tax residence.

Beyond the day count

Living in Brazil permanently is a separate route. A Brazilian returning with the intention of settling there can also become resident on arrival. Acquiring permanent status or a Brazilian employment relationship during a visit can establish residence before day 184. Becoming non-resident again involves separate departure rules; a brief exit does not reverse residence.

FAQ

Is Brazil’s threshold 183 or 184 days?

The day-count route starts on the 184th day within a period of up to 12 months. Exactly 183 days does not meet this route, although another residence test may apply.

Does leaving Brazil reset the count?

No. Separate visits can add together within the same arrival-based 12-month period.

Can I become resident on arrival?

Yes. Arrival with permanent residence or to work under a Brazilian employment contract can establish tax residence immediately.
