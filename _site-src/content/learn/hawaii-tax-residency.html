Hawaii's 200-Day Tax Residency Presumption

More than 200 days in Hawaii raises a rebuttable residency presumption. An outside permanent home and a temporary or transitory purpose are both part of rebutting it.

Last verified: 12 November 2026

In short: Hawaii does not have a hard statutory-resident line. Instead, being present in Hawaii for more than 200 days of the taxable year, counted in the aggregate, creates a rebuttable presumption that you were a resident from the time of your arrival. You can overcome it by proving you keep a permanent place of abode outside Hawaii and were in the state only for a temporary or transitory purpose.

The rule

Hawaii treats you as a resident in two situations: if you are domiciled in Hawaii, or if you are in Hawaii for other than a temporary or transitory purpose. The 200-day rule is not a separate residency test on its own. It is a presumption that pushes the second question in the state's favour once you cross a day count:

  • More than 200 days. If you have been in Hawaii more than 200 days of the taxable year, in the aggregate, you are presumed to have been a resident of Hawaii from the time of your arrival.
  • The presumption is rebuttable. It is not automatic residency. You can overcome it with evidence satisfactory to the Department of Taxation that you maintain a permanent place of abode outside Hawaii and were in Hawaii for a temporary or transitory purpose.

Without a successful rebuttal, crossing 200 days supports resident treatment even if you are domiciled elsewhere. That treatment can reach income from outside Hawaii; the tax calculation and any filing obligation still depend on the applicable income-tax rules.

How the presumption works

  1. Count every day on which you were present in Hawaii during the taxable year.
  2. Add the days across the whole year. They are counted in the aggregate and do not need to be consecutive.
  3. If the total is more than 200 days, the presumption of Hawaii residency applies from your date of arrival.
  4. To avoid resident treatment, rebut the presumption with evidence of a permanent home outside Hawaii and a temporary or transitory purpose for your stay.

Example. You are domiciled in California but spend 215 days across the year in Hawaii, working remotely from a long-term rental. The days are spread over several separate visits.

Assuming no special status rule applies, all 215 days are included in the aggregate. Crossing 200 days raises the presumption from arrival. Your California domicile alone does not rebut it: you also need evidence of an outside permanent abode and a temporary or transitory purpose.

Rebutting the presumption

The purpose of the stay matters more than a label such as snowbird or temporary worker. The rules give an example of a New York engineer assigned to a fixed 18-month Hawaii project. The engineer exceeds 200 days but rebuts the presumption by showing an outside permanent abode and the temporary assignment. Another example treats an indefinite employment transfer as residence. A long stay can therefore still have a temporary purpose; keeping a mainland home alone is not enough.

Beyond the day count

The 200-day presumption is only one route. Hawaii can also tax you as a resident by domicile, which turns on where your true, permanent home is rather than a day count, and by the general test of being in the state for other than a temporary or transitory purpose regardless of the exact number of days. Staying at or under 200 days avoids this time-based presumption, but does not by itself prove nonresidence. The statute also says residence is not gained or lost solely because of presence or absence under military or naval orders, aviation or navigation work, or study. Those provisions have their own conditions. The taxable year can be a calendar or fiscal year.

FAQ

How many days can you spend in Hawaii before you are presumed a resident?

The time-based presumption arises after more than 200 days in the taxable year, counted in the aggregate, subject to special status rules. It is rebuttable. Domicile or a non-temporary purpose can establish residence even at fewer days.

Can you rebut Hawaii's 200-day presumption?

Yes. The presumption is rebuttable with evidence satisfactory to the Department of Taxation that you maintain a permanent place of abode outside Hawaii and were in the state only for a temporary or transitory purpose.

Do the 200 days have to be consecutive?

No. The days are counted in the aggregate across the taxable year and do not need to be consecutive.
