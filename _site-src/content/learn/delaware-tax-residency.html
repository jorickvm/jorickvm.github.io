Delaware's 183-Day Residency Rule

A place of abode in Delaware plus more than 183 days in the state in a tax year makes you a Delaware resident, even if your domicile is elsewhere. Here is how the count works.

Last verified: November 2026

In short: Delaware treats you as a resident if you maintain a place of abode in the state and spend, in total, more than 183 days of the taxable year there. Both conditions must be met, and the day condition is met on the 184th day. Delaware does not define a day for this test, so the cautious count includes every day you spend any part of in the state.

The rule

Delaware's code defines a resident in two parts: someone domiciled in Delaware, for the period of that domicile, and someone who meets the 183-day test. The test has two conditions that must both be met:

  • A place of abode in Delaware. A home you maintain in the state, such as a house or apartment.
  • More than 183 days. You spend, in the aggregate, more than 183 days of the taxable year in Delaware, so 184 days or more.

Meet both and Delaware treats you as a resident for the year, even if you are domiciled in another state.

How to count it

  1. Confirm you maintain a place of abode in Delaware.
  2. Count every day you spent in Delaware, including days you were there for only part of the day.
  3. Add the days across the taxable year, 1 January to 31 December for most people. They do not need to be consecutive.
  4. If the total reaches 184 and you keep the abode, Delaware treats you as a resident for that year.

Example. You are domiciled in Pennsylvania and keep a beach house in Rehoboth Beach, staying from 1 May to 31 October. Counting both travel days, that is 184 days in Delaware.

With a place of abode and more than 183 days, Delaware treats you as a resident for the year. Leaving on 30 October instead keeps you at 183, which is not more than 183.

Beyond the day count

Delaware also has a rule for its own domiciliaries who work abroad. A Delaware domiciliary is not treated as a resident for a period in which they are in a foreign country or countries for at least 495 full days in any 18 consecutive months, are in Delaware for no more than 45 days in that period, and do not keep a permanent place of abode in Delaware where their spouse, children or parents are present for more than 45 days. It does not apply to employees of the United States, including members of the armed forces.

FAQ

How many days can you spend in Delaware without becoming a resident?

Up to 183 days in the taxable year. The test needs more than 183 days together with a place of abode in Delaware.

Do days alone make you a Delaware resident?

No. Without a place of abode in Delaware the 183-day test does not apply, though domicile can still make you a resident.

What is Delaware's 495-day rule?

An exception for Delaware domiciliaries abroad: at least 495 full days in foreign countries in any 18 consecutive months, with no more than 45 days in Delaware, takes them out of residency for that period. It does not apply to US government employees.
