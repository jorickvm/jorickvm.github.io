Cyprus's 183-Day Tax Residency Rule

More than 183 days in a calendar year makes you a Cyprus tax resident, and a separate 60-day rule can make you resident too. Here is exactly how the count works.

Last verified: 12 November 2026

In short: you become a Cyprus tax resident if you spend more than 183 days in Cyprus during a calendar year. Count arrival days in and departure days out. A same-day visit counts in; leaving and returning on the same day counts out. This is not simply a count of nights. Exactly 183 counted days stays below this test; 184 meets it. A separate 60-day rule can also make you resident on as few as 60 days if you meet extra conditions.

The rule

Cyprus treats you as a tax resident for a year if you stay there for one or more periods that in total exceed 183 days in that tax year. The tax year is the calendar year, 1 January to 31 December. Two points decide most real cases:

  • The window is the calendar year. The 183 days are measured over a single calendar year, not a rolling 12-month period. The count resets on 1 January.
  • Arrival counts in, departure counts out. The day you arrive in Cyprus counts as a day in the country, and the day you leave counts as a day outside it. If you arrive and depart on the same day, that counts as one day in; if you depart and later arrive on the same day, that counts as one day out. Those same-day rules mean a night count alone can be wrong.

How to count it

  1. List every Cyprus trip in the calendar year with arrival and departure dates.
  2. Include arrival days and exclude departure days. Count a same-day arrival and departure as in Cyprus; count departure and return on the same day as outside Cyprus.
  3. Add up those days across all your Cyprus stays in the same calendar year.
  4. If the total exceeds 183 days, the main day-count test is met and you are a Cyprus tax resident for that year.

Example. Say you fly into Cyprus and, counting the arrival day in but the departure day out, your stays for the year add up to exactly 183 days.

That is not enough: the rule requires more than 183 days. If the statutory day tally is 183, the main test is not met. One more counted day takes it to 184 and meets this test. Check same-day movements separately instead of substituting a total of nights.

Beyond the day count

The 183-day count is one route in, not the only one. Cyprus also has a 60-day rule, and the two tests are alternatives: meeting either one makes you a tax resident. Under the 60-day rule you are resident for the year if, in that same year, you meet all four of these conditions: you stay at least 60 days in Cyprus; you do not stay more than 183 days in any other single country; you carry on a business in Cyprus, are employed in Cyprus, or hold an office with an entity that is tax resident in Cyprus at any time during the year; and you maintain a permanent home in Cyprus that you either own or rent. Until 2025 a fifth condition applied, not being a tax resident of any other country; the 2026 tax reform removed it with effect from 1 January 2026. If the qualifying Cyprus business, employment or office-holding ends during the year, the 60-day route no longer applies for that year. If you fail any one of the four, the 60-day rule does not apply, though you can still be resident under the 183-day rule. And if another country also claims you as resident, a double-tax treaty decides the matter through tie-breaker rules such as permanent home and centre of vital interests.

FAQ

How many days can you stay in Cyprus without becoming a tax resident?

The main test is more than 183 counted days in a calendar year. The separate 60-day test can establish residence earlier, so 183 days is not a general guarantee of nonresidence.

What is the Cyprus 60-day rule?

An alternative test: you are resident on as few as 60 days if, in the same year, you stay at least 60 days in Cyprus, spend no more than 183 days in any other single country, carry on a business in Cyprus, are employed in Cyprus, or hold an office with an entity that is tax resident in Cyprus, and keep a permanent home in Cyprus that you own or rent. All four conditions must hold, and ending the qualifying Cyprus business, employment or office-holding during the year prevents residence under this route for that year. A fifth condition, not being tax resident elsewhere, was removed from 1 January 2026.

Is the Cyprus rule based on the calendar year?

Yes. The Cyprus tax year is the calendar year, and both the 183-day rule and the 60-day rule are measured over it rather than a rolling 12-month window.
