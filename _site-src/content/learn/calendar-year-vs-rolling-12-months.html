Calendar Year vs Rolling 12 Months: How to Count Travel Days

The same trips can produce different totals. Choose the counting window before comparing your days with a limit.

Last verified: November 2026

A calendar year has fixed boundaries. A rolling period moves with the date being checked. A fiscal year has its own fixed start, while a per-stay limit follows one visit. The threshold alone does not tell you which window applies.

Four windows to distinguish

Calendar year
1 January through 31 December of the same year. Days in different calendar years belong to separate totals.
Fiscal or tax year
A fixed annual period defined by the relevant system. A year running from 1 July through 30 June crosses two calendar years.
Rolling period
A moving window ending on the date being checked, or another interval specified by the rule. Yesterday's boundary is not today's boundary.
Per stay
A count for one visit. Separate cumulative limits or admission conditions may still apply.

The same trip under different windows

Take a hypothetical continuous visit from 1 December 2026 through 31 January 2027. For this arithmetic example, count both arrival and departure. There are 62 days: 31 in December and 31 in January.

A calendar-year count allocates 31 days to 2026 and 31 to 2027. A rolling 12-month window from 1 February 2026 through 31 January 2027 contains all 62. A fiscal year from 1 July 2026 through 30 June 2027 also contains all 62. A per-stay count is 62 if the relevant rule uses that inclusive method.

The calendar-year subtotal changes at New Year. The trip itself continues, and a rolling count still includes December. This is why leaving a country or crossing 1 January does not, by itself, establish that an allowance has reset.

180 days, 365 days and 12 months are different

A 180-day window ending on 31 January 2027 starts on 5 August 2026, when both boundary dates are included. Its start is the check date minus 179. Move the check date forward by 1 day and the start moves forward by 1 day too.

A calendar-month interval is not always a fixed number of days. The 12 months from 1 March 2027 through 29 February 2028 contain 366 days. Do not replace a legal 12-month period with 365 days, or six months with 180 days, unless the governing rule defines it that way.

Some rules test every day or every relevant interval, rather than one snapshot at year-end. For the regional example, the Schengen rolling-window walkthrough shows why checking the arrival date alone is insufficient for a planned stay.

Check the window before the threshold

  1. Find the exact period in the official rule: calendar year, fiscal year, fixed days, calendar months or one stay.
  2. Check which dates count: any part of a day, a midnight, a full day or another definition.
  3. Include every relevant stay inside the period, even if it started before the current year.
  4. Apply the threshold and any other conditions only after the count is correct.

FAQ

Does a rolling year reset on 1 January?

No. A rolling window moves with the date being checked. Days from the previous calendar year remain included until they fall outside that window.

Are 12 months always 365 days?

No. Calendar-month periods can include a leap day. Use the wording and boundary convention of the relevant rule rather than substituting a fixed day count.

Can one trip count in two calendar years?

Yes. Allocate each date to its calendar year. A separate rolling or fiscal-year calculation can include both portions of that same trip.
