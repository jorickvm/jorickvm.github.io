Cambodia Tax Residency: Counting 183 Days

Cambodia’s presence test uses more than 182 days in a 12-month period ending in the tax year. A domicile or principal home can establish residence on fewer days.

The threshold and the period

Cambodia’s current salary-tax regulation defines residence through domicile, a principal place of abode, or presence exceeding 182 days during a 12-month period ending in the current tax year. The presence need not be continuous. This is not simply a January-to-December counter.

Travel days count

The regulation treats presence during part of a day as a whole day. Include arrival and departure dates, and count an overlapping date only once. Use actual visits rather than visa validity dates or months rounded to 30 days.

A year boundary does not clear the record

Suppose distinct visits total 100 days late in one year and 83 days early in the next, all inside the same 12-month period. That period reaches 183 even though neither calendar-year total does. Conversely, a current rolling total falling below 183 does not by itself undo a residence position already established for a tax year.

Beyond physical presence

Domicile and a principal home are independent routes, so a shorter travel history does not prove non-residence. Keep travel evidence alongside housing and employment records. The day total identifies a presence threshold; it does not determine every income-tax consequence.

A 183-day example across two years

Illustrative dates: 1 September to 31 December 2025 contributes 122 days. Continuing from 1 January to 2 March 2026 adds 61 days. The total is 183 within one 12-month period, even though the separate calendar-year totals are only 122 and 61.

Set Count up to to 2 March 2026 to inspect that window. If the same arrival or departure is entered twice, it still represents one physical day, not an extra qualifying day.

Read a rolling result as a dated result

A rolling total needs an end date to be meaningful. “183 days in Cambodia” is less useful than “183 days in the 12 months ending 2 March 2026.” Keep the period with any result you save or discuss. Otherwise, two people can get different totals from the same trips simply because they checked on different dates.

As old visits fall outside the window, the current number can decrease. That is a change in the window’s arithmetic, not an automatic amendment of a tax return or a determination that residence has ended.

Check missing records before the threshold

  • Include visits before 1 January when they fall inside the chosen 12 months.
  • Include both travel dates and same-day visits under the partial-day convention.
  • Check whether a long stay was split into overlapping records.
  • Keep housing and domicile information separately, because those routes are not assessed by a presence counter.

For planning, enter the intended end of a future stay and choose that date as the window end. The result includes a plan, so it should not be presented as evidence of days already spent.

FAQ

Does Cambodia use 183 days in a calendar year?

The presence test uses a 12-month period ending in the tax year, so visits on both sides of New Year can combine.

Do arrival and departure count?

Yes. The current salary-tax regulation counts part of a day as a whole day.

Is 182 days enough for the presence test?

No. The rule says more than 182 days, so the first whole-day total meeting it is 183.
