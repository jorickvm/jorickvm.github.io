Albania's 183-Day Tax Residency Rule

Albania’s day-count test is 183 days or more in a calendar year. Check the separate residence routes and the special foreign-artist exception.

Last verified: 12 November 2026

In short: presence of 183 days or more in a calendar year is one route to Albanian tax residency, so the line is crossed on day 183 rather than day 184. The income tax law includes arrival and departure dates. A permanent home, the activity or habitual-abode criterion, or your centre of vital interests can establish residence separately. A special 24-month exception applies to artists who are not Albanian nationals.

The rule

Albania treats you as a resident taxpayer if you stay in the country during the tax year for a period, or several periods, totalling 183 days or more. For people outside the foreign-artist exception, three points decide the day-count test:

  • The threshold is inclusive. The law says 183 days or more, so a year with exactly 183 days of presence meets this test.
  • Both ends of a trip count. The statute includes the day of departure and the day of entry as days of presence. The instruction also includes physical-presence days such as working days and holidays.
  • The window is the calendar year. The Albanian tax year begins on 1 January and ends on 31 December, so the count resets each year.

How to count it

Albania counts calendar days of physical presence, not nights, and the periods do not need to be continuous.

  1. List every Albania trip with its arrival and departure dates.
  2. Count every day on which you were in the country, including the arrival day and the departure day.
  3. Add the stays together within a single calendar year.
  4. If the year total reaches 183, the day-count test is met.

Example. You arrive on 1 May and leave on 30 October, staying continuously.

Counting both dates, that is 183 days and meets the ordinary day-count criterion. Leaving one day earlier would make it 182, below this criterion. This assumes the foreign-artist exception does not apply.

Beyond the day count

A permanent home in Albania, the activity or habitual-abode criterion, significant personal or economic ties, and qualifying Albanian government service abroad are separate residence routes. The activity or habitual-abode route has its own exception: a permanent home outside Albania for the whole tax year and no permanent home in Albania. Staying below 183 days does not settle these other routes. If another country also treats you as resident, check any applicable double-tax treaty.

Article 8 also gives artists who are not Albanian nationals a 24-month non-resident exception. The current instruction says the clock starts with their first entry for artistic activity and continues through temporary departures; leaving and re-entering does not restart it. Keep evidence of the qualifying entry and stay. One published wording discrepancy remains: Instruction 26 paragraph 8.4 says more than 183 days, while the statute and the tax authority’s resident guidance say 183 or more. The statutory inclusive threshold is used here.

FAQ

How many days can you stay in Albania without becoming a tax resident?

Under the ordinary day-count test, 182 days is below the threshold and 183 meets it. Other residence routes can apply below that total, while qualifying foreign artists have a special exception.

Is Albania's rule based on a rolling 365-day period?

No. The current income tax law uses the tax year, defined as 1 January to 31 December, rather than a rolling 365-day period.

Do arrival and departure days count in Albania?

Yes. The law includes the arrival and departure dates as days of presence.
