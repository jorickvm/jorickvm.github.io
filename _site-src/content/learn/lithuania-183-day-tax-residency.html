Lithuania's 183-Day Tax Residency Rule

Lithuania has an annual 183-day tax-residence test and a 280-day test across two years. Both can matter even when no single stay is long.

Last verified: August 2026

In short: presence of 183 days or more in a calendar year is one route to Lithuanian tax residency, so the line is crossed on day 183 rather than day 184. Arrival and departure days both count, and any part of a day counts as a whole day. Lithuania also has a second numerical test that spans two consecutive years, and staying under 183 days in every single year does not clear it.

The rule

Lithuania treats you as a tax resident if you are present in the country, continuously or intermittently, for 183 days or more during the tax period. Three points decide most real cases:

  • The threshold is inclusive. The law says 183 days or more, so a year with exactly 183 days of presence meets this test. Many summaries say "more than 183 days", which is one day out.
  • Arrival and departure dates count. VMI includes both ends of a trip, along with working days, weekends, public holidays and holidays spent in Lithuania. Specific exclusions and add-backs can change the total.
  • The tax period is the calendar year. Count from 1 January to 31 December, then also check the adjacent-year test below.

How to count it

Lithuania counts calendar days of presence, not nights, and separate stays add together across the year.

  1. List every Lithuania trip with its arrival and departure dates.
  2. Count every day on which you were in the country, including the arrival day and the departure day.
  3. Add the qualifying dates within a single calendar year, counting an overlapping date only once. Apply any relevant exclusions or add-backs below.
  4. If the adjusted year total reaches 183, the annual day-count criterion is met. Special status exceptions and treaties need separate review.

Example. You spend 110 days in Lithuania in the spring, then return for another 73 days in the autumn.

Neither stay reaches the threshold alone, but the law adds them together: 110 plus 73 is 183, and 183 is enough.

The two-year 280-day test

Lithuania has a second numerical test, and it is the reason a year-by-year count can give you a false sense of safety. You are also a Lithuanian tax resident if you are present in the country for 280 days or more across two successive tax periods, provided that in one of those two years you were present for 90 days or more. The 280 figure is the total across both years, not a figure for either one, and VMI’s ordinary arrival examples treat both years as resident years. Special exceptions and the rules for permanent departure can affect that result.

Spending 120 qualifying days in one year and 160 in the next reaches 280 in total. VMI gives this exact example and treats both years as resident years, even though neither year reaches 183. Keep both years’ records: a later stay can change the tax-residence position for the earlier year.

Beyond the day count

A permanent home or stronger personal, social or economic interests in Lithuania can establish residence separately. Lithuanian citizens whose foreign pay or living costs come from state or municipal budgets also have a specific route. Exceptions cover certain foreign diplomats, foreign-government employees and non-citizens whose sole purpose is individual activity through a permanent base. Permanent-departure rules can preserve residence below 183 days, sometimes only until departure. An applicable tax treaty can change the result if another country also treats you as resident.

Day-count adjustments need evidence. Transit between two foreign countries is normally excluded if it lasts no more than 48 hours; documented forced stops have their own exception. Specified illness or family bereavement preventing departure can be excluded for up to 14 calendar days, with an inpatient-treatment extension in the illness case. Business trips from a Lithuanian workplace and qualifying employer-granted leave abroad can add days back, although leave after which you do not return is treated differently.

A separate exclusion covers qualifying Ukrainian citizens, family members and stateless people displaced by the war. It starts on registration with Lithuania’s Migration Department and applies for the period defined in the special rule. Other residence criteria can still apply.

FAQ

How many days can you stay in Lithuania without becoming a tax resident?

A total of 182 qualifying days is below the annual threshold; 183 meets it. That does not settle residence: the 280-day test, home or interests and permanent-departure rules can apply separately.

What is Lithuania's 280-day rule?

It combines at least 280 qualifying days across two successive calendar years with at least 90 in one of them. VMI’s 120-plus-160 example makes both years resident years, subject to the applicable exceptions.

Do arrival and departure days count in Lithuania?

Yes. VMI includes both arrival and departure dates, as well as weekends and holidays spent in Lithuania. Apply any specific exclusion or add-back before comparing the total with the thresholds.
