Iowa's 183-Day Residency Presumption

A place of abode in Iowa plus more than 183 days in the state in a tax year presumes you are an Iowa resident, even if your domicile is elsewhere. The presumption can be rebutted. Here is how the count works.

Last verified: November 2026

In short: Iowa taxes as a resident anyone who maintains a permanent place of abode in Iowa, whatever their domicile. If you keep a place of abode in Iowa and spend more than 183 days of the tax year in the state, Iowa presumes it is a permanent place of abode. The presumption can be rebutted.

The rule

Iowa residents are people domiciled in Iowa, and people who maintain a permanent place of abode in Iowa. Whether a place of abode is permanent depends on the time you spend in the area, the nature of the place, your activities there and your intentions. The 183-day rule turns that into a presumption:

  • A place of abode in Iowa. A house, apartment, condominium, mobile home or other dwelling you maintain or occupy, owned or rented.
  • More than 183 days. You spend more than 183 days of the tax year in Iowa, so 184 days or more.

Meet both and Iowa presumes you maintain a permanent place of abode there, which makes you a resident unless you rebut it. Presence that is not voluntary, such as confinement to a correctional facility or an extended hospital stay, may not create the presumption.

How to count it

  1. Confirm whether you keep a place of abode in Iowa.
  2. Count every day you spent in Iowa, including days you were there for only part of the day.
  3. Add the days across the tax year, 1 January to 31 December for most people. They do not need to be consecutive.
  4. If the total reaches 184 and you keep the abode, Iowa presumes you are a resident for that year.

Example. You are domiciled in Arizona and keep a farmhouse in Iowa that you live in from planting to harvest, arriving on 1 April and leaving on 1 October. Counting both travel days, that is 184 days in Iowa.

With a place of abode and more than 183 days, Iowa presumes the farmhouse is a permanent place of abode, which makes you a resident unless you can show otherwise. Leaving on 30 September instead keeps you at 183.

Beyond the day count

Iowa also presumes domicile, at any day count, when you keep a place of abode in Iowa and claim a homestead credit, are registered to vote in Iowa, keep an Iowa driver's license, or do not live in an abode in another state for more days of the year than in Iowa. It presumes you are not domiciled in Iowa when you claim no Iowa homestead credit, are not registered to vote in Iowa, hold no Iowa driver's license, are in Iowa for fewer than 183 days, and live in an abode elsewhere for at least 183 days. Being away from Iowa for 183 days does not by itself show that you have left an Iowa domicile.

FAQ

How many days can you spend in Iowa without being presumed a resident?

Up to 183 days in the tax year. Past 183 days, with a place of abode in Iowa, the presumption arises.

Do you need a home in Iowa for the 183-day rule to apply?

Yes. The presumption needs a place of abode in Iowa, owned or rented. Without one there is no presumption on days alone, though domicile can still make you a resident.

Can the Iowa presumption be rebutted?

Yes. It is a presumption that the place of abode is permanent, judged on your time there, the kind of place, your activities and your intentions. Involuntary presence, such as an extended hospital stay, may not create it.
