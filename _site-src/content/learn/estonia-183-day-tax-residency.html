Estonia's 183-Day Tax Residency Rule

Estonia uses 183 days or more in 12 consecutive calendar months. A place of residence can establish tax residence separately, and the authority requires you to report changes.

Last verified: 12 November 2026

In short: you become an Estonian tax resident if you stay in Estonia for 183 days or more during any period of 12 consecutive calendar months. Partial days count, including arrival and departure. The tax authority describes a narrow exclusion when a person who had to leave could not do so for a good reason beyond their control, such as serious illness. The window is not the calendar year, and having your place of residence in Estonia can make you resident even below 183 days.

The rule

Estonia treats you as a tax resident if you stay there for at least 183 days within any period of 12 consecutive calendar months. Two points decide most real cases:

  • The window rolls. The 183 days are measured across any 12 consecutive calendar months, not 1 January to 31 December. Two stays in different calendar years can combine inside one window.
  • Partial days count. Arrival and departure days count, however short the visit. Check the authority’s narrow exception where departure was prevented for a good reason beyond your control, such as serious illness, before excluding any days.

How to count it

  1. List every Estonia trip with arrival and departure dates.
  2. Count each day of presence, including arrival and departure. Review any claimed inability-to-leave exception with the tax authority.
  3. Total the days inside a single 12-month window, then slide that window across your whole travel history.
  4. If any 12-month window reaches 183 days, the day-count test is met.

Example. 100 days in Estonia between September and December, then 100 more between February and May the following year.

No single calendar year hits 183. But both stays sit in the same rolling window (1 September to 31 August the following year) and total 200 days, so the test is met.

Beyond the day count

The 183-day count is one route in, not the only one. Estonia also treats you as resident if your place of residence is in Estonia, meaning the place where you permanently or primarily live, regardless of the day count. Estonian diplomats in foreign service can also qualify. Once the presence test is met, residence can be registered retrospectively from the first certified arrival in the relevant 12-month period. Notify the Tax and Customs Board of residence changes using form R. If another country also treats you as resident and an applicable tax treaty exists, the treaty position must be assessed separately.

FAQ

How many days can you stay in Estonia without becoming a tax resident?

For this day-count route, stay below 183 counted days in every relevant 12-month period. A place of residence in Estonia or qualifying diplomatic service can establish residence separately.

Is the 183-day rule based on the calendar year?

No. It uses any 12 consecutive calendar months, so a stay split across two years can still reach the line.

Can you be resident with fewer than 183 days?

Yes, if your place of residence is in Estonia, meaning the place where you permanently or primarily live.
