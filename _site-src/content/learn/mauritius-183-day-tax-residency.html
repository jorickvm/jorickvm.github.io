Mauritius's 183-Day Tax Residency Rule

Mauritius uses a July-to-June income year. Check both the 183-day annual test and the 270-day total across three income years, alongside domicile.

Last verified: August 2026

In short: you become a Mauritian tax resident if your days in Mauritius reach 183 or more in a single income year (1 July – 30 June). A second, separate test also makes you resident if your days add up to 270 or more across the current income year and the two preceding ones. Days do not need to be consecutive under either test.

The rule

Mauritius treats you as a tax resident under either of two day-count tests. Meeting just one is enough:

  • The 183-day test. If your days present in Mauritius reach 183 or more within a single income year, you are a resident for that year. The window is the income year, 1 July to 30 June, not the calendar year.
  • The 270-day test. Even if no single year reaches 183, you are a resident if your days add up to 270 or more across the current income year and the two income years before it. This catches steady, sub-183-day presence repeated year after year.

Under both tests, separate stays in the relevant period are added together. MRA ruling TR55 confirms that arrival and departure days are included. Domicile is an independent route: you are also resident if domiciled in Mauritius, unless your permanent place of abode is outside Mauritius. Staying below both day thresholds does not rule that route out.

How earlier stays affect a later year

The 270-day test uses three specific income years: the year being tested and its two predecessors. It is not any rolling 36-month window. Earlier stays remain relevant while their income year is inside that window, so leaving after a long stay does not mean you can ignore the aggregate test.

A year with no Mauritius presence needs separate confirmation. Section 73 refers to presence in the income year being tested and the two preceding years. The official material linked below does not explain the result for a tested year with no presence at all. Do not assume a large earlier total automatically settles residence for that departure year; confirm that situation with the MRA.

How to count it

  1. Fix the income year: it runs 1 July to 30 June, so a day in, say, August belongs to the year that started that 1 July.
  2. Count each day present in Mauritius, including arrival and departure days, once. Add separate visits across the income year.
  3. If the total is 183 or more in that income year, you are a Mauritian tax resident for it.
  4. Also add the current income year and the two before it. A total of 270 or more meets the aggregate threshold. If the tested year has no Mauritius presence, get confirmation of that case before relying on the result.

Example. You spend 100 days in Mauritius in the 2024–25 income year, 95 days in 2023–24, and 90 days in 2022–23.

No single income year reaches 183 days, so the first test is not met. But 100 + 95 + 90 = 285 days across the three years, which is more than 270, so the second test makes you a Mauritian tax resident for the 2024–25 income year.

Why residency matters

Residence affects the scope of Mauritian income tax. Section 5 covers residents’ income from Mauritius and elsewhere, while subsection 5(3) treats an individual’s foreign income as derived when received in Mauritius, or dealt with there in the individual’s interest or on the individual’s behalf. This goes beyond a simple bank-transfer test. Non-residents can still have Mauritian tax obligations, and an applicable treaty may change the treatment of particular income or resolve dual residence for treaty purposes.

FAQ

How many days can you stay in Mauritius without becoming a tax resident?

There is no universal safe day limit. Fewer than 183 days stays below the annual test, but the 270-day aggregate and domicile routes still need checking. A year with no Mauritius presence also needs confirmation before you rely on an earlier-year total.

What is the income year for Mauritius tax residency?

It runs from 1 July to 30 June, not the calendar year. Days are added up across that window for the 183-day test, and across three such windows for the 270-day test.

What is the 270-day rule in Mauritius?

The alternative test looks at 270 days in aggregate across the current income year and the two preceding ones. If the tested year has no Mauritius presence, confirm the treatment before relying on prior-year days alone; this guide does not treat that case as a definitive residence result.
