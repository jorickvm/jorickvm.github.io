North Carolina's 183-Day Residency Presumption

Being present in North Carolina for more than 183 days in a tax year presumes you are a North Carolina resident. The presumption can be rebutted. Here is how the count works.

Last verified: November 2026

In short: North Carolina residents are people domiciled in the state, or living there for other than a temporary or transitory purpose. On top of that, anyone present in North Carolina for more than 183 days of the taxable year is presumed to be a resident, unless there is convincing proof to the contrary. No home in the state is needed for the presumption.

The rule

North Carolina has no automatic day line. Its test is whether you were domiciled in the state, or resided there for other than a temporary or transitory purpose. The 183-day rule is a presumption laid over that test:

  • More than 183 days. If you are present in North Carolina for more than 183 days of the taxable year, so 184 days or more, you are presumed to be a resident.
  • Rebuttable. Convincing proof that your stay was temporary and your domicile is elsewhere can overcome the presumption.

Below the line, the presumption does not arise, but domicile or a non-temporary stay can still make you a resident.

How to count it

  1. Count every day you were in North Carolina, including days you were there for only part of the day.
  2. Add the days across the taxable year, 1 January to 31 December for most people. They do not need to be consecutive.
  3. If the total reaches 184, North Carolina presumes you are a resident for that year.

Example. You are domiciled in Florida and spend spring to autumn in a mountain house near Asheville, arriving on 1 April and leaving on 1 October. Counting both travel days, that is 184 days in North Carolina.

That is more than 183, so North Carolina presumes you are a resident. Your Florida domicile and the seasonal nature of the stay are the proof you would offer against it. Leaving on 30 September instead keeps you at 183, and no presumption arises.

Beyond the day count

The presumption only runs one way. Being away from North Carolina for more than 183 days raises no presumption that you are not a resident. A resident who moves away stays a North Carolina resident until they have both established a definite domicile elsewhere and abandoned their North Carolina domicile.

FAQ

How many days can you spend in North Carolina without being presumed a resident?

Up to 183 days in the taxable year. Past 183 days the presumption arises, though domicile can make you a resident at any day count.

Is North Carolina's 183-day rule automatic?

No. It is a presumption that convincing proof to the contrary can overcome, such as a domicile clearly elsewhere and a stay that was only temporary.

Does spending more than 183 days away make me a nonresident?

No. Absence for more than 183 days raises no presumption that you are not a resident. You remain one until you establish a definite domicile elsewhere and abandon the North Carolina one.
