Arkansas's Six-Month Residency Rule

A permanent place of abode in Arkansas plus more than six months in the state in a tax year makes you an Arkansas resident, even if your domicile is elsewhere. Here is how the count works.

Last verified: November 2026

In short: Arkansas treats you as a resident if you maintain a permanent place of abode in Arkansas and spend, in the aggregate, more than six months of the tax year in the state. Both conditions must be met, and exactly six months does not qualify. Arkansas does not say how many days make six months, so the cautious reading treats anything past 180 days as more than six months.

The rule

Arkansas residents are people domiciled in Arkansas, and people who meet the six-month test. That test has two conditions that must both be met:

  • A permanent place of abode. A place where you have established a permanent home, even if you are away from it for long periods. A temporary home does not count, because a place of abode needs some degree of permanence.
  • More than six months. You actually spend, in the aggregate, more than six months of the tax year in Arkansas. Less than six months, or exactly six, does not qualify.

How to count it

  1. Confirm you maintain a permanent place of abode in Arkansas.
  2. Count every day you spent in Arkansas, including days you were there for only part of the day.
  3. Add the days across the tax year, 1 January to 31 December for most people. They do not need to be consecutive.
  4. Arkansas states the rule in months, not days. If your total is past 180 days and you keep the abode, treat it as more than six months.

Example. You are domiciled in Tennessee and keep a house on a lake in the Ozarks, staying from 1 April to 1 October. Counting both travel days, that is 184 days in Arkansas.

That is more than six months, and with a permanent place of abode, Arkansas treats you as a resident for the year. Keeping your total at 180 days or fewer leaves you clear of the line.

Beyond the day count

Domicile is a separate route. Arkansas treats domicile as a place of physical presence combined with the intent to make it your permanent home, and you have only one at a time. Moving somewhere you intend to stay only temporarily, however long, does not change it.

FAQ

How long can you stay in Arkansas without becoming a resident?

Up to six months of the tax year, in total, even with a permanent place of abode there. Exactly six months does not qualify, and staying at 180 days or fewer is the cautious course.

Do you need a home in Arkansas for the six-month rule to apply?

Yes. The test needs a permanent place of abode in Arkansas as well as more than six months. A temporary home does not count.

Does exactly six months make you an Arkansas resident?

No. Arkansas's rules say a person who spends less than six months, or exactly six months, in the state does not fall within the test.
