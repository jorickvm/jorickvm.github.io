When Do You Get Schengen Days Back After a 90-Day Stay?

When you can return after using 90 days, with a dated example and the distinction between today’s balance and a future stay.

Last verified: 11 November 2026

Quick answer: after 90 uninterrupted days outside Schengen, the 90/180 rule allows a new stay of up to 90 days, provided you meet the other entry conditions. For shorter gaps or several scattered trips, check every day of the planned stay. Old days can drop out of the rolling window while you are back inside Schengen.

After 90 days in, how long must you stay out?

If you used 90 consecutive days, spend the next 90 days entirely outside Schengen before returning for another full 90-day stay. Entry and exit days both count as days inside, so the first full day outside is the day after departure.

Example in 2026. You enter on 1 January and leave on 31 March, using 90 days. You remain outside from 1 April through 29 June, another 90 days.

You can return on 30 June for a stay through 27 September, subject to your visa and other entry conditions. On each day of this new stay, an old day falls outside the 180-day window as a new day is added. The total never exceeds 90.

Why one available day can support a longer planned stay

On 30 June in that example, only one day of the old trip has fallen outside the window. A snapshot can therefore show just one unused day before you add the new arrival. That does not mean you must leave the next day: another old day drops out tomorrow, and the same continues throughout the planned stay.

The rule checks the total on each day, not just the balance on entry. Distinguish days unused on one date from the length of a future stay that remains compliant throughout.

What if you made several shorter trips?

With scattered visits, old days drop out in the pattern of those visits. A shorter gap may be enough for another trip, but you need the actual dates to calculate how long it can last.

  1. List every short-stay entry and exit in Schengen.
  2. For each day of the proposed trip, count that day and the preceding 179 days.
  3. Count both arrival and departure days. Keep the total at 90 or fewer throughout.

The Schengen calculator helps check your dated trips. For the general rule and its scope, see the Schengen 90/180 guide.

A day-count allowance is only one entry condition

A single-entry visa may already be used up, a visa may authorize fewer days, and its validity dates can shorten the trip. The 90/180 calculation does not override those conditions or guarantee admission. Stays authorized under a residence permit or long-stay visa are treated separately from ordinary short stays.
