Slovakia's 183-Day Tax Residency Rule

Slovakia uses at least 183 days in a calendar year. Registered permanent residence or a qualifying dwelling can establish residence below that line.

Last verified: 12 November 2026

In short: presence of at least 183 days in a calendar year is one route to Slovak tax residency, so the line is crossed on day 183 rather than day 184. Every started day counts, however short, which means arrival and departure days both count. Registered permanent residence or a qualifying dwelling are independent domestic residence criteria. The solely-study or treatment exception and any applicable treaty need separate review.

The rule

Slovakia treats you as habitually staying in the country, and therefore as a tax resident, if you stay there at least 183 days in the relevant calendar year, whether continuously or across several periods. Three points decide most real cases:

  • The threshold is inclusive. The law says at least 183 days, so a year with exactly 183 qualifying days meets this test. There is no need to reach 184.
  • Every started day counts. Any part of a day, however short, is a day of presence. Both the arrival day and the departure day count, and so does a single-day visit.
  • The window is the calendar year, and only the calendar year. The Slovak tax authority states explicitly that the 183 days are measured in the relevant calendar year and not in any twelve-month period, so the count resets on 1 January.

How to count it

Slovakia counts calendar days of physical presence, not nights, so a stay of a single afternoon still adds a day to the total.

  1. List every Slovakia trip with its arrival and departure dates.
  2. Count every day on which you were in the country, including the arrival day and the departure day.
  3. Add the stays together across a single calendar year. They do not need to be consecutive.
  4. If the year total reaches 183, the domestic day-count criterion is met. Review the solely-study or treatment exception and any applicable treaty before deciding the final status.

Example. You spend 100 days in Slovakia between January and April, then return for another 83 days between September and November.

Neither stay comes close on its own, but the law adds them together: 100 plus 83 is 183, and 183 is enough. The day-count criterion is met exactly. This example assumes the solely-study or treatment exception does not apply; treaty treatment is separate.

Beyond the day count

Registered permanent residence in Slovakia and a qualifying dwelling are independent domestic residence criteria. A dwelling must be available for more than occasional accommodation, with the surrounding personal and economic circumstances showing an intention to live there permanently. Staying below 183 days does not settle those other criteria. A person habitually staying solely for study or medical treatment is treated as a non-resident under a specific exception.

For the 183-day route, the Financial Directorate’s guidance treats residence as applying throughout the tax period, rather than starting on day 183. Residence based on changes to a home or registered residence can have different timing. If another country also treats you as resident and an applicable double-tax treaty exists, its residence provisions must be considered separately.

FAQ

How many days can you stay in Slovakia without becoming a tax resident?

Under this day-count criterion, 182 days is below the threshold and 183 meets it. Registered permanent residence or a qualifying dwelling can establish residence separately, while the specific exception and treaty position still need review.

Do arrival and departure days count in Slovakia?

Yes. Every started day of stay counts, however short, so both ends of a trip count and a same-day visit counts as a full day.

Can you be a Slovak tax resident with fewer than 183 days?

Yes. Registered permanent residence or an available dwelling with an intention to live there permanently can meet an independent domestic criterion. The solely-study or treatment exception and any applicable treaty can affect the final status.
