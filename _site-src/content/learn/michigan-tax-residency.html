Michigan's 183-Day Residency Rule

Living in Michigan for 183 days or more in a tax year makes you a Michigan resident, even if your home is in another state. No home in Michigan is needed. Here is how the count works.

Last verified: November 2026

In short: Michigan treats anyone who lives in the state for at least 183 days of the tax year as a resident domiciled in Michigan. Unlike most states with a day rule, Michigan attaches no home condition: the days alone are enough. Michigan does not define a day for this test, so the cautious count includes every day you spend any part of in the state.

The rule

Michigan taxes its residents on all their income, and a resident is someone domiciled in Michigan: the fixed, permanent home you always intend to return to. The Income Tax Act adds a second way in. An individual who lives in Michigan for at least 183 days during the tax year is treated as a resident domiciled in the state, wherever their home is.

  • 183 days is enough. The line is met on the 183rd day, not the 184th.
  • No home is needed. Most states with a day rule, New York among them, also require a home in the state. Michigan's test is the days alone.
  • Short tax years. For a tax year of less than 12 months, the line is more than half of its days.

How to count it

  1. Count every day you spent in Michigan, including days you were there for only part of the day.
  2. Add the days across the tax year, 1 January to 31 December for most people. They do not need to be consecutive.
  3. If the total reaches 183, Michigan treats you as a resident for that year.

Example. You are domiciled in Florida and spend every summer at a lake cottage in Michigan, arriving on 1 May and leaving on 31 October. Counting both travel days, that is 184 days in Michigan.

At 183 or more, Michigan treats you as a resident for the year, despite your Florida domicile. Leaving on 29 October instead keeps you at 182.

Beyond the day count

Most Michigan residency questions turn on domicile, not days. Once established, a Michigan domicile is kept until you intend to abandon it, intend to take up a specific new one, and are actually present in the new state. A Michigan retiree who winters in Florida therefore stays a Michigan resident until the move is real, however few days they spend in Michigan. Treasury's residency rule applies the 183-day count where the domicile factors do not settle the question. Several Michigan cities, Detroit among them, levy their own income tax with residency rules of their own.

FAQ

How many days can you spend in Michigan without becoming a resident?

Up to 182 days in the tax year, if your domicile is elsewhere. At 183 days Michigan treats you as a resident domiciled in the state.

Do you need a home in Michigan for the 183-day rule to apply?

No. Unlike most states with a day rule, Michigan's test counts only the days you live in the state.

Does a winter away end Michigan residency?

No. If Michigan is your domicile, a temporary absence does not change it. You stay a Michigan resident until you intend to leave for good, intend to make a specific new state your home, and are actually living there.
