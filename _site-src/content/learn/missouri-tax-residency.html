Missouri's 183-Day Residency Rule

A permanent place of abode in Missouri plus more than 183 days in the state in a tax year makes you a Missouri resident, even if your domicile is elsewhere. Here is how the count works.

Last verified: November 2026

In short: Missouri treats you as a resident if you are not domiciled there but maintain a permanent place of abode in Missouri and spend more than 183 days of the taxable year in the state. Both conditions must be met. A second rule works the other way: a Missouri domiciliary who lives elsewhere can be a nonresident by spending 30 days or fewer in Missouri.

The rule

Missouri starts from domicile: if Missouri is your permanent home, you are a resident. Someone domiciled elsewhere becomes a resident under the 183-day test, which has two conditions that must both be met:

  • A permanent place of abode in Missouri. A home you maintain in the state.
  • More than 183 days. You spend, in total, more than 183 days of the taxable year in Missouri, so 184 days or more.

Meet both and Missouri treats you as a resident for the year.

How to count it

  1. Confirm you maintain a permanent place of abode in Missouri.
  2. Count every day you spent in Missouri, including days you were there for only part of the day.
  3. Add the days across the taxable year, 1 January to 31 December for most people. They do not need to be consecutive.
  4. If the total reaches 184 and you keep the abode, Missouri treats you as a resident for that year.

Example. You are domiciled in Kansas and keep a lake house at the Lake of the Ozarks, staying from 1 April to 1 October. Counting both travel days, that is 184 days in Missouri.

With a permanent place of abode and more than 183 days, Missouri treats you as a resident for the year. Leaving on 30 September instead keeps you at 183, which is not more than 183.

Beyond the day count

Missouri's statute also gives domiciliaries a way out. Someone domiciled in Missouri is not a resident for the year if they maintain no permanent place of abode in Missouri, maintain a permanent place of abode elsewhere, and spend no more than 30 days of the year in Missouri. For someone who has moved away but not yet changed domicile, that makes the Missouri day count matter in both directions.

FAQ

How many days can you spend in Missouri without becoming a resident?

Up to 183 days in the taxable year, if your domicile is elsewhere. The test needs more than 183 days together with a permanent place of abode in Missouri.

Do days alone make you a Missouri resident?

No. Without a permanent place of abode in Missouri the 183-day test does not apply, though domicile still makes you a resident.

What is Missouri's 30-day rule?

A Missouri domiciliary is not a resident for the year if they keep no permanent place of abode in Missouri, keep one elsewhere, and spend no more than 30 days in the state.
