Spain Long-Term Residence: The 12-Month Absence from the EU

For this long-term-residence rule, the relevant absence is from the European Union. Counting only days outside Spain, or treating every Schengen country as part of the EU, changes the answer.

Last verified: September 2026

In short: Spain's standard long-term-residence provision includes 12 consecutive months outside the EU as a ground for loss of the authorisation. Certain former EU Blue Card holders and qualifying family members have a 24-month period instead. The legal period is calendar-based.

Status covered
Spanish long-term residence, standard absence branch
Relevant absence
12 consecutive calendar months outside the EU
Blue Card alternative
24 months for the qualifying cases specified in Article 201
Relevant territory
EU member states, the French outermost regions and Åland
Outside this territory
EEA-only countries, Switzerland and associated overseas territories
Legal basis
Real Decreto 1155/2024, Article 201; procedure in Article 202

The rule

The regulation distinguishes the place that issued the residence authorisation from the territory used for this absence test. Spain issued the status, but Article 201's standard continuous-absence provision refers to the European Union. A stay in another EU country therefore belongs on a different side of the travel record from a stay outside the EU.

  • The EU and Schengen are different sets. Norway and Switzerland participate in Schengen but are not EU members. Substituting Schengen territory would change which stays count as EU presence.
  • The standard and Blue Card branches differ. The longer period is tied to the specified long-term resident–EU cases following Blue Card status, including qualifying family members. It is not an allowance that every long-term resident can choose.
  • Loss of authorisation has a procedure. Articles 201 and 202 describe a decision by the competent authority and a procedure with an opportunity to be heard. A day total alone does not establish the outcome of that procedure.

The EU territory includes the French outermost regions and Åland. It excludes associated overseas territories that are outside the EU, as well as Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway and Switzerland. The Canary Islands are part of Spain; the Azores and Madeira are part of Portugal.

How to count it

Identify the branch applicable to the status first. Then construct the continuous-absence record from stays inside the relevant EU territory, keeping the legal calendar period separate from a fixed day total.

  1. Confirm whether the standard 12-month provision or the specified 24-month alternative applies.
  2. Record the date you left the EU territory and each subsequent return, including stays elsewhere in the EU.
  3. For a complete-day illustration, treat EU departure and return dates as presence and count the dates between them as away. Check the authority's treatment of any disputed travel day.
  4. Check the continuous calendar period against the rule. Do not add unrelated absences together or replace twelve months with a fixed 365-day deadline.

Example. Your last EU stay ends in Spain on 1 October 2025. You next stay in France from 1 to 5 April 2026, then remain outside the EU until returning on 1 October 2026.

With the travel dates recorded as EU presence, the first absence contains 181 days, from 2 October through 31 March. The second contains 178 days, from 6 April through 30 September. The record shows separate absences, not one continuous 359-day absence.

If the April stay were in Norway instead of France, there would be one uninterrupted EU absence of 364 days between the two October travel dates. Those dates are twelve calendar months apart. That demonstrates both distinctions: the country set changes the record, and a 365-day allowance is not an exact twelve-month deadline.

Beyond the day count

Article 201 also contains other grounds for loss of the authorisation. For long-term resident–EU status it separately addresses six years outside Spanish territory. Keeping the EU absence within one allowance therefore does not resolve every long-term-residence condition, and that separate Spain-specific period needs its own record.

Where an absence may already affect the authorisation, check the procedure and any applicable recovery route with the competent authority. This guide also concerns retaining long-term residence, not acquiring it, becoming a Spanish citizen or qualifying as a tax resident. Those are separate questions even when they use the same travel log.

FAQ

Does a stay in France count as absence from the EU?

No. France is inside the EU. This continuous-absence provision concerns the EU territory, rather than Spain alone.

Can I use the Schengen country group instead?

No. The sets differ. For example, Norway and Switzerland are in Schengen but outside the EU, so substituting that group would change the absence calculation.

Does everyone with long-term residence get 24 months?

No. Article 201 assigns the longer period to specified former EU Blue Card holders with long-term resident–EU status and qualifying family members.
