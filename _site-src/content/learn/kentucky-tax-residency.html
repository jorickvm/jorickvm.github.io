Kentucky's 183-Day Residency Rule

A place of abode in Kentucky plus more than 183 days in the state in a tax year makes you a Kentucky resident, even if your domicile is elsewhere. Here is how the count works.

Last verified: November 2026

In short: Kentucky treats you as a resident if you are not domiciled in Kentucky but maintain a place of abode there and spend more than 183 days of the taxable year in the state. Both conditions must be met. Once you are a resident, the wage agreements Kentucky has with neighboring states no longer apply to you.

The rule

Kentucky residents are people domiciled in Kentucky, and people who are not domiciled there but are caught by the 183-day test. That test has two conditions that must both be met:

  • A place of abode in Kentucky. A home you maintain in the state.
  • More than 183 days. You spend, in total, more than 183 days of the taxable year in Kentucky, so 184 days or more.

Meet both and Kentucky taxes you as a resident for the year.

How to count it

  1. Confirm you maintain a place of abode in Kentucky.
  2. Count every day you spent in Kentucky, including days you were there for only part of the day.
  3. Add the days across the taxable year, 1 January to 31 December for most people. They do not need to be consecutive.
  4. If the total reaches 184 and you keep the abode, Kentucky treats you as a resident for that year.

Example. You are domiciled in Ohio and rent an apartment in Louisville for a long work assignment. Across the year you are in Kentucky on 190 days.

With a place of abode and more than 183 days, Kentucky treats you as a resident, and the Ohio wage agreement described below no longer covers your pay.

Beyond the day count

Kentucky has reciprocal agreements with Illinois, Indiana, Michigan, Ohio, Virginia, West Virginia and Wisconsin, under which wages earned in Kentucky by a resident of one of those states are taxed by the home state instead. The Department of Revenue's instructions say that people who live in Kentucky for more than 183 days during the tax year are residents, and reciprocity does not apply. So a commuter who drifts past 183 days loses that protection as well.

FAQ

How many days can you spend in Kentucky without becoming a resident?

Up to 183 days in the taxable year. The test needs more than 183 days together with a place of abode in Kentucky.

Do days alone make you a Kentucky resident?

No. Without a place of abode in Kentucky the 183-day test does not apply, though domicile can still make you a resident.

Does the reciprocity agreement still apply after 183 days?

No. The Department of Revenue treats people who live in Kentucky for more than 183 days during the tax year as residents, and reciprocity does not apply to them.
