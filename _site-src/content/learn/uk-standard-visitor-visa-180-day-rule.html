UK Visitor Stay Limit: Six Months per Visit

Most Standard Visitors can stay for up to six calendar months per visit. There is no automatic 180-days-per-year allowance, and repeated visits must still be genuine visits.

Last verified: 12 November 2026

In short: a Standard Visitor can stay in the UK for up to 6 months per visit. There is no published rolling window and no "180 days per year" rule; the popular "6 months in 12" shorthand does not appear in the Immigration Rules. Alongside each stay's length, Border Force and caseworkers assess whether you are a genuine visitor, so visits that add up to living in the UK can be refused even when no single stay exceeds 6 months.

The rule

The UK's visitor limit is a per-visit allowance, not a rolling window. Under the Standard Visitor route, each visit carries its own permission to stay:

  • Up to 6 months per visit. This is the usual maximum, not a guaranteed grant. Narrow exceptions include up to 11 months for private medical treatment and up to 12 months for qualifying academics. Your own permission controls the date you must leave.
  • No rolling formula. The often-quoted "180 days per year" or "6 months in any 12" test is not in the rules; the UK runs no Schengen-style 90/180 calculation.
  • The genuine-visitor test sits on top. Every entry must satisfy an officer that you intend a genuine visit and will leave at the end of it. The day count alone does not settle that.

How to count it

  1. Check the period granted in your visitor permission. Record your arrival date and the date by which you must leave.
  2. Treat six months as calendar months, not a fixed 180 days. Leave by the expiry of your permission.
  3. Keep a dated record of every visit over the previous 12 months; that pattern, not a formula, is what gets assessed at your next entry.

Example. You enter the UK on 10 March as a Standard Visitor.

Suppose your permission requires you to leave by 9 September. Use that date, rather than adding 180 days. If you leave in June and seek entry again in October, admission and the length of any new permission are assessed again, taking your earlier visits into account.

Leaving and coming back

Because there is no published formula, the pattern of your visits does the work a formula would. The caseworker guidance tells decision makers to weigh the number and length of visits over the previous 12 months, the gaps between them, whether you spend more time in the UK than in your home country, and whether successive visits amount to living in the UK through repeat entries. Three assumptions cause most of the trouble:

  • "Leaving resets a fresh full stay." The next entry is judged against your wider pattern, not in isolation.
  • "A long-term visit visa settles it." Long-term visit visas still permit only genuine visits, not de facto residence.
  • "Only long trips matter." Many short trips with brief absences can read as living in the UK just as easily as one long stay.

Beyond the day count

The ETA is a pre-travel permission to board, not a stay allowance; the 6-month framing and genuine-visitor test apply to the stay itself regardless. Spending many days in the UK can also make you UK tax resident under a separate set of rules with its own article: see the UK Statutory Residence Test.

FAQ

Is there a 180-days-per-year rule for UK visitors?

No. The published rule is up to 6 months per visit; no "6 months in any 12" formula appears in the Immigration Rules. A pattern of visits that adds up to living in the UK can still lead to refusal.

Does leaving the UK reset your 6 months?

No fresh stay is guaranteed. Re-entry is a new assessment: officers consider earlier visits, gaps between them, and whether repeated visits amount to living in the UK. Any new permission sets your next departure deadline.

Does an ETA let you stay longer in the UK?

No. The Electronic Travel Authorisation is permission to travel to the UK, not a stay allowance. Visitors with an ETA are still subject to the Standard Visitor rules, including the up-to-6-months limit and the genuine-visitor test.
