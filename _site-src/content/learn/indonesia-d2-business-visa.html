Indonesia D2 Business Visa: Stay Length and Extensions

The D2 multiple-entry business visa allows an initial stay of up to 60 days per arrival. Approved extensions can take one stay to 180 days in total.

Last verified: 19 November 2026

The visa and the stay are different

D2 is a multiple-entry business-visit visa. Its overall validity lets you seek entry while the visa is valid; it does not let you remain for that whole period on one visit. Immigration’s D2 announcement describes up to 60 days per arrival.

Extensions are separate approvals

Immigration’s current guidance for multiple-entry visitor stay permits provides extensions in 60-day increments, up to 180 days in total, with separately listed exceptions for other visa types. Start with the permission actually granted. An application or an intention to extend is not an approved additional allowance.

Keep the dates for each visit

Record arrival, the expiry of the stay permit and departure. Keep each extension approval with the original permission. For example, an initial 60-day stay followed by an approved additional 60 days gives a total of 120 days for that stay, not a fresh 120 days from approval.

Business visits are not employment permission

Follow the activities authorised by the visa and the expiry stated on your stay permit. Do not substitute the visa’s overall validity for that date, or use a business-visit record as evidence that local employment is permitted. A later entry is a new immigration event and should be recorded separately.

How an extension changes the count

Illustrative dates: treating 1 March 2026 as day one, a 60-day stay ends on 29 April. If an extension grants another 60 days, the total becomes 120 days from the original arrival, ending on 28 June under that same inclusive convention.

An approval received on 15 April does not make 15 April the arrival date. Keep 1 March as the start and use the new expiry on the permit. The example is arithmetic; your issued stay permission remains the authority.

Three dates that are easy to mix up

Visa validity
The period in which the visa can be used under its terms. It is not the duration of a single stay.
Stay-permit expiry
The deadline for the current stay, including an approved extension.
Planned departure
Your travel plan. It does not extend either document.

If these dates disagree, resolve the document question before choosing a convenient number in a calculator. A multiple-entry visa can still have a much shorter allowance for each visit.

A useful extension log

For each entry, retain the arrival record, initial stay expiry, extension application date, approval and revised expiry. Record departure as a separate event. This makes it possible to explain why a stay was longer than the original 60 days without presenting every later visit as if it had the same extension.

The business label on a calendar entry is not evidence of which visa you used. If your history contains visits under different permissions, check the document for each entry rather than applying D2 retrospectively to the entire country history.

FAQ

Can I stay for the entire validity of the visa?

No. Visa validity and the permission for each stay are different. Follow the stay-permit expiry.

Is 180 days automatic?

No. The initial allowance is up to 60 days. A longer stay requires approved extensions.

Should I restart my count when an extension is approved?

No. An extension increases the total allowance for the existing stay. Keep its original arrival date.
