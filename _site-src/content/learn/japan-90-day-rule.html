Japan's 90-Day Rule Explained

Most visa-exempt travelers can stay in Japan for up to 90 days per entry as a Temporary Visitor. Here is what the 90 days covers, who gets less, and what happens when you leave and come back.

Last verified: August 2026

In short: under Japan's visa exemption, most of the 74 visa-exempt nationalities receive Temporary Visitor status for up to 90 days per entry. Indonesia and Thailand get 15 days, Brunei and Qatar 30 days. It is a per-entry grant, not a rolling window: no formula limits your total days across visits, but officers can refuse entry when a pattern looks like living in Japan on tourist stays.

The rule

Japan's 90-day allowance is a per-entry grant attached to Temporary Visitor status, not a rolling counter like Schengen's 90/180. Three things decide what you actually get:

  • Nationality sets the maximum. Most of the 74 visa-exempt nationalities can be granted up to 90 days per entry. Indonesia and Thailand are limited to 15 days, Brunei and Qatar to 30.
  • Landing permission controls the stay. The immigration officer examines each arrival and grants landing permission showing your status of residence and period of stay. That permission, not the exemption list, is the legal basis for your time in Japan.
  • Temporary Visitor covers short visits only. The status is for tourism, family visits, business meetings and similar purposes, and does not permit paid work.

How to count it

  1. Check the period of stay on the landing permission placed in your passport on arrival.
  2. Count from the day you land. Arrival day counts as a day in Japan.
  3. Leave by the expiry of your period of stay. There is no default beyond what the permission states.

Example. You land in Tokyo on 1 April and receive a 90-day period of stay.

1 April is day 1, so your stay runs through 29 June, day 90. If your landing permission had said 15 or 30 days instead, that shorter period would be your limit, whatever the general rule for your nationality.

Leaving and coming back

Each departure ends that entry's grant, and each return is a fresh landing examination. Japan publishes no rolling window and no formula for how long to wait between visits, so no number guarantees re-entry, and none forbids it. The pattern is what matters: back-to-back near-90-day stays with short absences can lead an officer to conclude you are effectively living in Japan, and landing permission can be refused on that basis. Track your cumulative days rather than assuming each exit resets a clean slate.

Beyond the day count

If your nationality is not on the exemption list, you need a visa before travel; eligible nationals can apply online for short-term tourism through MOFA's JAPAN eVISA system. For anything beyond an ordinary short visit, or an unusual travel pattern, rely on current official guidance rather than this summary.

FAQ

How long can you stay in Japan without a visa?

Most of the 74 visa-exempt nationalities can stay up to 90 days per entry as a Temporary Visitor. Indonesia and Thailand get 15 days, Brunei and Qatar 30. The period on your landing permission is what counts.

Does Japan have a 90/180 rule like Schengen?

No. Japan's limit is per entry, and no rolling window or annual cap is published. Immigration officers can still refuse entry when repeated visits look like de facto residence.

Does leaving Japan reset the 90 days?

Each new entry brings a fresh landing examination and a fresh period of stay, but there is no published formula and no guaranteed reset. Frequent long stays with short gaps can lead to refusal at the border.
