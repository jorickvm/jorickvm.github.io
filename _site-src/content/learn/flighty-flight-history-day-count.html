How to Turn Your Flighty History Into a Visa and Residency Day Count

Export your flights from Flighty as a CSV, import the file into AtlasDays, and review the trips it builds.

Last verified: September 2026

In short: Flighty logs your flights, but it does not count days per country. Export your Flighty history as a CSV and import it into AtlasDays, which turns the flights into dated trips and counts them against Schengen 90/180, 183-day rules, and the rest.

Export your flights from Flighty

  1. Open Flighty and go to the Profile tab.
  2. Tap Settings (the gear icon).
  3. Scroll to Account Data and tap it.
  4. Tap Export Your Flights.
  5. Save the CSV file to the Files app, for example in iCloud Drive or On My iPhone. Do not open and resave it in a spreadsheet first.

This is the menu path as of September 2026. Flighty added the export in version 4.3, and the labels may move in a later update.

The export only contains flights that are in Flighty. If your older trips never made it in, add them before you export: Flighty imports flights from the past year for free, while older flights require Flighty Pro.

Import it into AtlasDays

  1. Open AtlasDays and go to Settings → Import → Flighty.
  2. Tap Select Flighty CSV and choose the file you saved.
  3. AtlasDays parses the file on your iPhone and shows a preview with counts for layovers, overnight flights, incomplete trips, duplicates, and overlaps. Tap any of them to review those trips.
  4. Deselect anything you do not want, then tap Import and confirm.

Screenshots of each screen are in Help Center: Flighty Import. The import is free; only splitting US trips by state needs AtlasDays Pro. What it does with your flights:

  • Airports become countries. An arrival in a country and the next departure from it bracket one stay, and domestic flights in between stay inside that trip.
  • Stops under 10 hours become Transit, which adds no days to any count. Stops of 10 to 24 hours become a stay with a long-transit warning, so you decide. See Does a Layover Count as Visiting a Country?
  • Flight details go into the trip notes: the date, time, route, and flight number behind each stay. Canceled flights are skipped.
  • Nothing is saved until you approve it. Trips missing an arrival or departure flight are listed as incomplete, so you add the date instead of accepting a guess.

Before you rely on the count

  • Ground crossings are invisible. A train, ferry, or drive across a border never appears in a flight log, so set those dates by hand.
  • Check the trips that feed a limit. One misread layover or missing leg changes a Schengen 90/180 total, so review every stay inside the window you care about.
  • Mind the gaps before your Flighty history. Years you never logged in Flighty need filling from other records before a multi-year total means anything.

FAQ

Does Flighty count your days in a country?

No. Flighty logs your flights and builds flight stats, but it does not total the days you spend in each country or check them against a visa or residency limit. You need to turn the flights into dated stays first, which is what importing the Flighty CSV into a day-counting app does.

How do I export my travel history from Flighty?

As of September 2026, open the Profile tab in Flighty, tap Settings, then Account Data, then Export Your Flights. Save the CSV file to the Files app and keep the original unedited, because a resaved spreadsheet may no longer match Flighty's format.

Does it cost anything to import Flighty flights into AtlasDays?

No. The Flighty import is part of the free version of AtlasDays, including the review of stays, transits, duplicates, and overlaps. Only tracking US trips state by state needs AtlasDays Pro.
