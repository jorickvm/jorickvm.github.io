Louisiana's Six-Month Residency Rule

Spending more than six months of a tax year in Louisiana, in total, makes you a Louisiana resident, even without a home there. Here is how the count works.

Last verified: November 2026

In short: Louisiana deems you a resident if you spend, in the aggregate, more than six months of the taxable year in the state. No home is needed: the time alone is enough, and the Department of Revenue treats such people as residents for the entire year. Louisiana does not say how many days make six months, so the cautious reading treats anything past 180 days as more than six months.

The rule

Louisiana residents are people domiciled in Louisiana, people who maintain a permanent place of abode there, and people who spend more than six months of the taxable year in the state. The six-month route stands on its own:

  • More than six months, in the aggregate. The months do not need to be consecutive. Exactly six months is not more than six.
  • No home condition. Time alone is enough. A home in Louisiana is a separate route to residency in its own right.

Louisiana's statute deems such a person a resident, and the Department of Revenue treats them as a resident for the whole year.

How to count it

  1. Count every day you spent in Louisiana, including days you were there for only part of the day.
  2. Add the days across the taxable year, 1 January to 31 December for most people. They do not need to be consecutive.
  3. Louisiana states the rule in months, not days. If your total is past 180 days, treat it as more than six months.

Example. You are domiciled in Texas and spend each winter and spring in New Orleans, from 1 January to 30 June. Counting both travel days, that is 181 days in Louisiana.

That is exactly six calendar months, which is not more than six. But Louisiana does not count in days, and 181 days is past the cautious line of 180, so leaving a few days earlier is the safer choice. Staying into July would put you past six months, and Louisiana would deem you a resident for the year.

Beyond the day count

Louisiana taxes residents on all their income and nonresidents on Louisiana income. Someone resident for less than six months of the year reports all income for the resident part and Louisiana income for the rest, and may file either the part-year return or the resident return. Only the resident return allows a credit for tax paid to another state on income earned while resident in Louisiana.

FAQ

How long can you stay in Louisiana without becoming a resident?

Up to six months of the taxable year, in total, if you have no domicile or permanent place of abode in Louisiana. Louisiana does not convert the months into days, so staying at 180 days or fewer is the cautious course.

Do you need a home in Louisiana for the six-month rule to apply?

No. Spending more than six months in the state is enough on its own. A permanent place of abode in Louisiana is a separate route to residency.

Does the six-month rule make you a resident for the whole year?

Yes. The Department of Revenue treats people residing in Louisiana for more than six months as residents for the entire year.
