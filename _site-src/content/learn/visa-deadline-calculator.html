How the dates are calculated

  • Official deadline. The calculator uses the date you enter without adjusting it. Check the admission record or permission, rather than assuming the visa expiry date is the departure deadline.
  • A number of days. Arrival is day 1. For example, a 30-day stay starting on 1 June ends on 30 June.
  • Calendar months and years. These move to the matching calendar date, not a fixed number of days. When the target month has no matching date, its final day is used. Choose whether your permission ends on that anniversary or the day before it.
  • Remaining time. The countdown uses today’s date on your device. Zero days means the deadline is today. No time of day or border time zone is inferred.

One entry’s duration is separate from a rolling stay limit. For Schengen or Türkiye, you must also consider earlier stays in the rolling window. Use the Schengen calculator or Türkiye calculator for that check.

Check the permission behind the date

For the United States, the I-94 admit-until date or D/S notation records the authorized stay. This calculator cannot turn D/S into a fixed departure date. For Canada, an officer can specify a shorter or longer stay, and document expiry can matter. Read the US visitor guide or Canada visitor guide before choosing a duration.

FAQ

Does this calculate my legal visa allowance?

No. You supply the deadline or permitted duration. The calculator does not determine nationality-based eligibility, extensions, residence status or any other permission condition.

Is six months always 180 days?

No. Calendar months have different lengths. Use an official departure date when available; otherwise confirm the counting convention that applies to your permission.

What happens to my dates?

They stay in your browser and disappear when you reload. The CSV download contains a deadline summary, not an AtlasDays trip-import file.
