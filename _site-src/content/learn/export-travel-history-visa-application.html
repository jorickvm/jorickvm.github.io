How to Export Your Travel History for a Visa Application

What an exported travel-history record is trying to achieve, when it genuinely helps, and what to verify before relying on one in a formal submission.

Last verified: June 2026

In short: an export, whether a CSV, a PDF, or a formatted list, presents a travel chronology. It does not validate one. Approximate dates, gaps, and quiet guesses survive the export in a cleaner format that looks more authoritative than the data behind it. Export is the step after building a reliable record, not a substitute for it.

When an export genuinely helps

  • A form asks for country-by-country trips across several years and you want a structured reference to copy from rather than relying on memory.
  • You have a consulate appointment and want a formatted summary of your travel alongside your passport.
  • You need to compare your record against another document, such as a prior application or an official entry record, before you submit.
  • You are preparing the same history for several purposes at once and need it presented consistently across different forms.

What it does not solve: a record that was never complete, because export moves information out of a log and does not add trips that were never entered; approximate or reconstructed dates, because export preserves the precision of the underlying data; and discrepancies between your log and your passport or official records, which exist in the data and remain after export.

What makes an exported record weak

  • Gaps in coverage. Trips logged inconsistently, or a log that only starts from a certain date, produce a partial record that looks complete.
  • Approximate dates presented as exact. A trip logged as "arrived June 1" when the date is uncertain exports as confirmed, and becomes a problem when compared against a passport stamp or official entry record.
  • Missing legs of multi-country trips. A journey through a third country or across several destinations may have been logged as a single trip. Whether the missing legs matter depends on what the application asks for.
  • No source notes on entries. A date with no note about what it rests on is harder to verify or correct later, and that missing context does not travel with the export.

Export quality is a downstream problem. If an export looks incomplete or uncertain, fixing the underlying log is the only way to produce a better one.

What to verify before you rely on one

  • Coverage. Does the record span the full lookback period the application requires? Most applications specify a window, and a log that starts later than that window is incomplete even if it is internally accurate.
  • Approximations. Are uncertain dates clearly marked? An estimate can conflict with a passport stamp or official entry record if the two are compared later.
  • Gaps. Are there unexplained periods with no activity? A gap inconsistent with the rest of the application may prompt questions.
  • Passport alignment. Do the key entries match the stamps and visas in your passport? Check the main trips before you submit.
  • Consistency with prior submissions. What you submit now should not contradict travel history you have already declared without explanation.

When exporting is not the problem

Exporting from a well-maintained log is straightforward. It stops being reliable when the log was built retrospectively from memory, when the record was spread across apps, spreadsheets, or documents that were never reconciled into one chronology, when earlier trips were logged with varying precision that looks identical once exported, or when the application asks for detail the log was never designed to capture, such as purpose of travel, city-level locations, or supporting-document references. In those cases the underlying record needs work before any export will be reliable, which the reconstruction page covers in detail.

Where official instructions take over

This page is a general explainer about exported travel records. It does not describe what any specific application requires or what any authority will accept. Applications differ in the detail they ask for: exact dates or approximate periods, country-level or city-level, all travel or only certain categories. Some ask for supporting documents alongside any travel summary, and an exported list is not itself documentation, so official supporting evidence such as passport stamps, booking confirmations, or official entry records may still be needed. Whether a formatted export is accepted as a supporting document, or is useful only as a reference for completing the form, depends on that application's instructions. The form's own guidance, the relevant authority's requirements, and where appropriate professional immigration advice control what to prepare and how to present it.
